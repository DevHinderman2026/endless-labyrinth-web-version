Accessibility Statement

Last updated: 4 November 2026.

1. Our aim

Endless Labyrinth is an audio game, made by a developer who is totally blind and tests it by ear. The aim is simple: the game and this website are fully playable and readable by blind and low-vision players, without sight, on every device the web version supports. Accessibility is not an extra here; it is the whole design.

2. How the game is played

Everything you need is in the sound: where things are, how far away they are, and whether they are in front of you or behind. The game speaks for itself with your device's own voice, so it asks you to turn your screen reader off while you play, and back on when you leave, and it tells you the shortcut at the start of every session. On a phone or tablet it is played with simple touch gestures; on a Chromebook, Mac or Linux computer, with the keyboard. Every gesture and key is listed in About and how to play.

3. Which screen reader to use

This website reads with the screen reader built into your device: VoiceOver on an iPhone, iPad or Mac, TalkBack on Android, ChromeVox on a Chromebook, and Orca on Linux. The Accessibility page names the right one for the device you are on and gives a setup guide for each.

4. How the site is built

  • Every page uses real headings, so you can jump between sections, and real lists, links and buttons.
  • The site links are the same on every page. On a narrow screen they fold behind a button named Site links menu, which announces whether it is expanded or collapsed.
  • Nothing depends on color, pictures or a mouse. Every control works from the keyboard and with a screen reader.
  • Status messages, such as the result of a backup or the events being checked, are announced as they appear.
  • Event descriptions are written for the keyboard and reworded with gestures on a phone, so instructions always fit your device.
  • The display options on the Accessibility page offer high contrast and bigger text for low-vision players, on every page and in the game.

5. Supported devices and known limits

  • Phones and tablets: iPhone and iPad (iOS 18.4 or newer) and Android phones and tablets with an up-to-date Chrome.
  • Computers: Chromebook, Mac and Linux, with the keyboard. Chrome, Edge and Safari are the browsers the game is tested with; Firefox should work but is not tested.
  • Windows computers are pointed to the full Windows version of the game, which supports NVDA and JAWS and a Braille display.
  • There is no Braille output during play, because the screen reader is off while you play.
  • On an iPhone or iPad, silent mode mutes the game's sounds while its voice still speaks, so the game asks for silent mode to be off.

6. Tell us what is wrong

If anything on this site or in the game does not work with your screen reader, Braille display or other assistive technology, please say so at the contact address in the footer of every page. Problems reported by players are fixed in the next version and listed in the Update History.
