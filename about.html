About and how to play

Endless Labyrinth, the web version.

What the game is

Endless Labyrinth is a 3D audio game: you play it by ear, with headphones. It began as a game for Windows computers. Since November 2026 the complete game also plays in a web browser, on a phone, a tablet or a computer: all six modes and the events of the Windows version, and nothing to install. The website also has six modes of its own: Ultimate Mode (web version 2.0.0), Find the Voice (6.0.0), The Abyss (6.2.0), Water Park (7.0.0), The Museum (7.2.0) and Story Mode (7.3.0); see below. Since web version 5.0.0 the whole web game is heard in binaural 3D sound and its walking modes turn with you; the Windows version is in stereo, with north always ahead. The rules of each mode are kept as close to the Windows version as the two can be, and both play the same events.

Want to know how the game came to be? The Story of the Game tells how it grew from a single labyrinth found by ear into six modes, events, a Windows release and this web version, and how I make it: designing and testing by ear, with Claude writing the code.

Supported devices

Phones and tablets, played with touch gestures: an iPhone or iPad with iOS 18.4 or newer, or an Android phone or tablet with an up-to-date Chrome.

Computers, played with the keyboard: a Chromebook, a Mac or a Linux computer. Chrome, Edge or Safari is recommended; Firefox should work, but the game is not tested with it. On a Mac, Safari needs macOS with Safari 18.4 or newer.

Windows computers play the whole game on the website too, from web version 5.0.0, with the keyboard. Endless Labyrinth also has a full Windows version to download, which works offline, with Braille display support: the Windows version.

How the sound works

Everything is placed by sound. From web version 5.0.0 the whole game is heard in binaural 3D, the browser's own head-related panning, so with headphones a sound is placed properly in front of you, behind you or to one side, not only left and right. Distance is loudness: the closer something is, the louder it gets. Something behind you also sounds duller, as if heard over your shoulder. In Standard, Battle, Tower, Island, Find the Voice, The Abyss, Water Park, The Museum, Story Mode, the walking levels of Chaos and Ultimate Mode the world turns with you: you turn where you stand, every sound sweeps round you as you do, the background included, and you walk the way you are facing. Driving, a road in Chaos, the events and an elevator ride do not turn: there north is always ahead. In the labyrinth, the battle arena, a tower and a road's tunnels, every sound has a slight echo of the place it is in, shaped by the walls around you and by the air of each level; islands, the seabed and the open road have none. In Ultimate Mode every area of the world has an echo sized to that area, and a sound can be below you as well. Every sound that has a place is played from one exact point, so it sits precisely where its thing is. Together these make a space you can move through by ear.

During a run the game speaks with its own voice, Bella, a natural American voice made by Kokoro, an open speech model, and heard with the echo of the room you are in. The menus, the help, Game Over and your final score keep your device's own voice. Every line Bella says is a recording that comes with the game's sounds, so she is always ready, and sounds the same on every device.

Starting

  1. Put on headphones and open the play page. On an iPhone or iPad, turn silent mode off: it mutes the game's sounds, though not its voice.
  2. Press Start. The first time, the game downloads its sounds, about 120 megabytes, and its voices, about 95 megabytes more, so use Wi-Fi if you can; after that they stay on your device.
  3. The game speaks for itself, so it asks you to turn your screen reader off. On an iPhone, triple-click the side button, and on an iPad the top button (on older models of either, the Home button), if your Accessibility Shortcut is set up, or ask Siri. On Android, hold both volume keys for three seconds if that shortcut is set up. On a Mac, press Command F5 for VoiceOver; on a Chromebook, Control Alt Z for ChromeVox; on Linux, Super Alt S for Orca.
  4. With the screen reader off, swipe up anywhere on the screen to begin, or on a computer press Enter.
  5. When you leave the game, turn your screen reader back on the same way.

Gestures

Swipe up or down
In a menu, move through it. In a run, one step the way you face, or one step straight back. In an event, where you do not turn, one step north or south.
Flick left or right
A quick turn of 30 degrees, in every mode that turns: Standard, Battle, Toy Memory, Volcanic Caves, Tower, Island, Find the Voice, The Abyss, The Edge, Water Park, The Museum, Story Mode, the walking levels of Chaos and Ultimate Mode. Three flicks take you from one main direction (north, east, south or west) to the next. In an event it is one step west or east, and in driving a lane change. In Volcanic Caves, lift your accelerating finger first.
Slide left or right and keep your finger down
In every mode that turns: you keep turning for as long as your finger stays on the screen, moving or resting, and stop exactly where you lift. A finger that lifts at once was a flick; one that stays down is a slide.
Slide up and hold
Run, during a run: you step forward at once, then keep stepping, fast (280 steps a minute), for as long as your finger stays on the screen.
Slide down and hold
Walk backwards, during a run: you step back at once, then keep stepping, more slowly (200 steps a minute), for as long as your finger stays on the screen.
Double tap
Choose, like Enter. During a run, the report: in standard mode your clock, and in the other modes everything that mode keeps track of, such as your ammo, kills and wave in battle. In driving, and in chaos's battles, roads, tower floors and islands, it also gives the seconds left on the clock; in chaos it ends with your points.
Two-finger tap
Attack, like the spacebar: the laser, a throw or a shot down the road.
Three-finger tap
Pause, and resume.
Triple tap
Back, in menus and while paused. While paused it asks whether to quit the run.
Four taps
Help, on the main menu and while paused.
Hold a finger still for a second
In Ultimate Mode only, the compass, on or off. In every other mode a finger resting still does nothing.
One tap
Nothing.

Only lifting your finger stops a run or a backward walk; you also stop by yourself at a wall, at the edge of an arena, and whenever a new level begins. On a touch screen you cannot turn while you run: lift your finger, turn, then run again. Lift it, too, before you shoot with two fingers or double tap. Running and walking backwards work in every mode and in events, except driving, which has its own accelerator, and an event where up and down are answers rather than steps.

Driving

Driving, in driving mode and on a chaos road, works its own way. Put a finger on the screen and slide it up: the car accelerates for as long as that finger stays on the screen. Lift it and the car coasts. Swipe down to brake: each swipe is one brake, and if your accelerating finger slides back down without lifting, it stops accelerating and brakes once. To change lanes, lift your finger and swipe left or right. To shoot or hear the report, lift first, then tap with two fingers or double tap; the car coasts meanwhile.

During a run only the double tap is counted, so it acts at once and an extra tap can never end a run by accident. The main menu has a Gesture Practice that says the name of every gesture it feels. A Bluetooth keyboard also works, with the Windows game's keys: the arrows, Enter, Space, Escape, P, H and T, and C for the compass.

Every topic from the game's own help, with a Play button for every sound, is on the Help page.

Keys, on a computer

On a Chromebook, Mac, Linux or Windows computer, the game uses the Windows game's keys, and everything it says names the keys instead of gestures. In the modes that turn, the left and right arrows turn you, a press for a quick turn of 30 degrees and a held arrow for as long as you hold it, and the up and down arrows step forward and back, or run and walk back while held. Gesture Practice is not on the menu.

Arrow keys
Move through a menu. In driving, hold the up arrow to accelerate, press the down arrow to brake, and left and right to change lanes. In events each arrow is a step north, south, west or east, with north always ahead. In Standard, Battle, Tower, Island, Find the Voice, The Abyss, Water Park, The Museum, Chaos and Ultimate Mode the left and right arrows turn you, a press for a quick turn of 30 degrees and a hold to turn freely, and the up and down arrows step the way you face and straight back; held down, they run and walk backwards, and you can turn while you run.
Enter
Choose. During a run, the report, as the double tap above.
Space
Attack: the laser, a throw or a shot down the road.
P
Pause, and resume.
Escape
Back, in menus. During a run, it asks whether to quit the run.
H
Help, on the main menu and while paused.
T
The time left on the clock.
C
The compass, in Ultimate Mode.

The game pauses by itself if you lock your device, switch apps or leave the page.

On an iPhone or iPad, the game's sounds dip while its voice speaks, and come back about a second after. That is how iOS mixes speech with other sound, and a web page cannot change it.

Standard mode

Find the exit of each labyrinth before the clock runs out. You start with 60 seconds, and every level you complete adds 7 seconds. The exit is marked by a beep that rises and quickens as you get closer. Walls give a sharp knock and you do not move. Time credits, marked by a spinning beacon, turn up in about a third of levels. Your score is the level you reach.

Battle mode

No walls and no exit: endless waves of enemies come at you. Whatever sounds centred between your ears is what your shot takes, in front of you or behind. Between waves comes a break, the only time ammo and time credits are laid out, one at a time: ammo for as long as the break lasts, and three to six time credits worth 15, 25 or 35 seconds; run dry in the middle of a wave and a single emergency cartridge appears, once a wave. The clock starts at 80 seconds. During a wave it runs only while an enemy is after you; during a break it never stops. Your score is the number of enemies you shoot down.

Toy Memory

Battle's sub mode: choose Battle Mode on the main menu, then Toy Memory. A gentle game of memory, with no clock and no crowd, over a calm fountain. A soft music box marks a box somewhere on the floor: turn and walk into it to open it, and a toy inside plays the sound of one of Battle's enemies, once only. Then enemies appear around you. They never hurt you or chase you; they sound one at a time, in turn, round and round, so you can hear where each one is. Turn to the one that matches the toy and shoot it, with a laser much narrower than Battle's. You have three lives: a wrong shot or a miss costs one, and the round goes on until you find the right enemy. There are two enemies at first, and one more every three boxes, up to six. Your score is the boxes you solve. Chaos Mode has Toy Memory levels too, where a wrong shot costs health instead of a life. New in web version 8.2.0; the website only.

Driving mode

A two minute run down an open road in one of three lanes. Time credits on the road buy more clock, which is the only thing that ends the run. A crash costs thirty seconds and all of your speed. Two high scores: cars destroyed, and distance in metres.

Volcanic Caves

Driving's sub mode: choose Driving Mode on the main menu, then Volcanic Caves. Inspired by Roller Ball in Sonar Islands. You drive down through volcanic caves, each level a cave deeper toward the centre of the Earth. Drive as on the road, accelerating with your finger held up and braking with a swipe down, but here the cave turns with you: lift your finger, then flick or slide left or right to turn. Each cave is a wide open cavern with no walls inside, only its edge. Find the hole by its beeping, faster and higher as you near it, and drive into it to drop to the next cave. The bombs, ticking like clocks, are the only thing to avoid: drive into one head on and you are back at the start of that cave, though brushing past does nothing. The edge stops you with a thud but costs no time. The lava of Escape the Cave is all around, louder the deeper you go, until it drowns out nearly everything. Two minutes to start, and a few seconds more for every hole, with no time credits; your score is the deepest level you reach. Chaos Mode has Volcanic Caves levels too, where a bomb costs health and time instead. New in web version 8.2.0; the website only.

Tower mode

Climb a tower one floor at a time. A rival guards each floor and races you for the items lying on it, then throws them at you; the stairs appear once you have destroyed it. Every tower is three to ten floors, and each one is one and a half times as hard as the last. There is no clock. Your score is the floors you climb.

Island mode

A chain of islands, and the only way across is down. Clear each island of enemies, ride its elevator to the seabed, find the key while your air lasts, and ride up to the next island. Nothing is described to you: every island sounds different, and everything that happens has its own sound.

Find the Voice

A guide calls to you from somewhere in a dark room, in a voice of her own: find her by ear. When you are within two steps of her a chime sounds, and she calls again from somewhere new; five finds complete a level, and on each level she stands farther away. A blind creature shares the room and hunts by sound: step on the broken glass scattered over the concrete floor and it charges, growling, until you are back on concrete. Three lives and no clock; your score is the levels you complete. New in web version 6.0.0.

The Abyss

Inspired by Papa Sangre. A huge, endless abyss with no walls and no edge, its whole floor broken glass, so every step can be heard. Blind creatures live all through it and hunt by sound: they come for you only while you move, creeping toward your steps when you walk and charging when you run; when you stop, they go back to wandering slowly, never toward you. Lightning strikes every few seconds, really loud, and draws them away from you; for five seconds after it your steps are not heard. Walking is soft and heard only close by; running is loud. Smash as many glass cases as you can before the clock runs out: each one adds five seconds, a little more as the run goes on, but the smash is loud, so smash just after the lightning. The clock starts at forty-five seconds, you have three lives, and your score is the cases you smash. Every three cases one more creature wakes, up to ten, the lightning comes less often, and new cases appear further away, with no end. Chaos Mode has Abyss levels too. New in web version 6.2.0; the website only.

The Edge

The Abyss's sub mode: choose The Abyss on the main menu, then The Edge. Inspired by the abyss level of Papa Sangre II. You cross the abyss on a gravel path one step wide, with only its wind around you and no creatures at all: the edge is the only danger. The ledge runs straight for one or two steps, never more, then bends a quarter turn left or right, and near the far side it opens out to the exit, the buzzer of The Museum's door, which you hear from the start. You cannot see the edge, so you throw glass, as much as you like: it lands one step ahead the way you face, and if it lands on the gravel with a clink, like a cup set down, that step is safe; if it whooshes away and breaks far below, that is the edge. Every step that brings you to the edge kicks a stone off it, and you hear it tumble away, a warning before you step again. You begin in the middle of a square of gravel, two steps from the edge on every side. Step off and you fall, screaming, and the run is over. You have five minutes for the whole run, with time credits of 20, 30 or 40 seconds lying on the path of about half the crossings, and your score is the crossings you finish, each with more bends than the last. Chaos Mode has Edge levels too: the chaos clock keeps running, and reaching the buzzer puts thirty seconds on it, or sixty after a fall. There a fall costs 35 health and 75 seconds, and lands you on the glass floor of the abyss below, by The Abyss's rules: smash glass cases, more in each Edge level of the run, up to fifteen, while the clock runs on, and an elevator carries you back up to where you fell. New in web version 8.0.0; the website only.

Water Park

A big water park that rings like a huge hall, heard by its busy pool and the children playing in it. Each park has a number of attractions, each found by its own sound, and each holds one token: win every token before closing time and you go straight on to the next park, which has one more attraction. The water slide is found by its staircase beacon: you climb by yourself, then ride straight down to the splash. At the diving pool you dive from the board and hold your breath while you swim to the token's bell on the bottom. In the wave pool you wade to the far side, standing still each time a wave breaks over you, or it carries you back. The tipping bucket fills, louder and louder, then dumps, and a bell says run under it now: stay a moment under it to take its token. Three bells, a second apart, count down to the next dump. At the duck stand, found by its carnival organ, a ding says start: ducks fly in at you from all around, and you turn and shoot them down with an air rifle; a duck that reaches you costs ten seconds. A fence runs all round the park, and buzzes when you walk into it, as Battle Mode's edge does. Nothing in the park can hurt you; a mistake brings the lifeguard's whistle, back to the edge of that attraction and five seconds off the clock. Each park has its own clock, and your score is the tokens you win. Chaos Mode has water parks too, where each token is ten seconds on the chaos clock. New in web version 7.0.0; the website only.

The Museum

Inspired by Papa Sangre. Each museum holds the soul of one of the characters of Ultimate Mode's safe zones, chosen at random, and you hear their own world playing softly through a great echoing hall. Their memories are scattered through it, each a little tune of its own that plays where it lies; about one in three is locked in a glass case, which you find by its hum and knocking and smash. Only one memory sounds at a time, its tune leading you to it, and only once the character has finished speaking; each memory you find gives the character back a piece of their life, which they tell you. Find them all and a buzzer sounds from the door: walk to it, and you walk through by yourself into the next museum, with another character and one more memory, up to eight. The one danger is the bottomless pools, which dissolve memory: they bubble where they lie, the character warns you when your next step straight ahead would take you in, and a fall ends the run at once (in Chaos Mode it costs 50 health instead, and you are pulled out). Creatures sleep in the museum and wake, each with a roar, only on noise: a smashed case, or the sparks of a fiery memory. Its firework bursts on your left or right, and then every step you take pops a spark just in front of you; the first pop wakes the creatures, and they charge until the last has popped, so keep moving. A creature that reaches you knocks you toward the nearest pool and leaves you one step from its edge. There is no clock, and your score is the souls you free. Chaos Mode has museums too, where the chaos clock keeps counting down while you find the memories. New in web version 7.2.0; the website only.

Story Mode

A walk through how Endless Labyrinth works, in twenty-three chapters, told by Bella, the game's own voice. It is first on the main menu. The first chapters are the basics: why you need headphones and how to centre a sound, turning with a flick and with a held slide, telling in front from behind, walking, running and walking backwards, walls, echo and the report. Then there is a chapter for every mode, from Standard Mode to Chaos Mode, with three for Ultimate Mode: the world, the compass, and the safe zones with their characters. Then Events, and last of all help and settings. Each chapter is a room with one small task: centring a bell, turning to north, walking a corridor, finding a labyrinth's exit by its beep, shooting enemies that stand still, changing lanes to let a car pass, climbing to the stairs, finding Bella by her voice, smashing a glass case, crossing from one place of Ultimate Mode into the next, and more. Bella tells you about each one first; only the room's background plays while she talks. When a task is done a door opens: find it by ear and walk through into the next chapter. Nothing can hurt you, there is no clock, and no task can be failed. Once, midway, not everything is so easy. About 35 minutes; once you have walked it to the end, it opens with a list of the chapters. New in web version 7.3.0, and twenty-three chapters long since 7.5.0; the website only.

Chaos mode

Every mode at once, and the hardest in the game. Each level is chosen at random from a labyrinth, a battle, a tower floor, a stretch of road, an island, a Find the Voice room, an abyss, a water park, a museum or, rarest of all, a whole world of Ultimate Mode, and nothing tells you which: you work it out from what you hear. Every run starts with exactly five minutes on one clock, and time credits add more; your score is points. One health bar lasts the whole run: it starts at 100, every finished level adds 10, and it never goes past 150. Now and then a treasure chest lies by a level's exit, with more time, health or points.

Ultimate Mode

The hardest mode in the game, made for the web alone (web 2.0.0) and the first that was never part of the Windows game. Chaos tests switching between modes against the clock; this tests survival. One huge, continuous world is built at random every run from five to ten areas of five kinds, each keeping its home mode's sounds: walled labyrinths, islands with a shore, sand and an elevator down to the seabed, tower areas with throwers racing you for items, battle areas with chasers, and safe zones, which sound like the places of the Windows game's events and have no enemies. There is no clock: you start with 100 health and the run ends when it reaches zero, with no hit ever taking more than 25. At a random moment, anywhere from two minutes to five into each world and with no warning but a five-second countdown, the whole world regenerates at random, and you must work out where you are again by ear; your ammo, tower items and health carry over, and everything you earn is worth more with every world you survive. Since version 4.0 the world turns with you: you turn where you stand, by quick turns of 30 degrees or a held turn that stops exactly where you let go, with a soft tick at each main direction and a higher one for north; the backgrounds turn round you with everything else; you walk exactly where you face, sliding silently along a wall you meet at a slant; and a shot takes the nearest enemy within 30 degrees either side of straight ahead. A compass, turned on by holding a finger still for a second (C on a keyboard), muffles the world and leads you along the walkable route to the nearest safe zone: its centred beep rises as you turn to face the way to go, and it names each direction you face and describes what lies that way. Since version 4.1 a water slide drops you into a pool you have to wade or swim out of, and here and there stands a humming glass case: smash it with your attack and what was inside is yours at once, but two to five fast chasers hear the glass break and come for you, to be shot or outrun. Since version 6.0.0 every safe zone has a character living in it, twenty-six in all, each with a voice of their own: walk to them and they tell you something about the world, a rumour or a hint, then give you a gift, such as ammo, health or tower items, and now and then something rarer. The compass now stays on for half a minute at most, then rests for fifteen seconds. The whole mode is heard in binaural 3D, with every sound placed around you and each area ringing with its own echo.

Events

Time-limited challenges, published for the Windows version and played here too, the very same events. When one is running it appears in the Events menu, with its own high scores. Each event is checked as genuine before it can be played.

The game's help, opened with four taps on the main menu, explains every rule of every mode and has demo sections that play every sound. Ultimate Mode has the most detailed section of all, with every area, the enemies, health, ammo, the credits and multiplier, the regeneration, the compass and the 3D sound.

Your scores

High scores are kept on your device, in your browser, and are never sent anywhere. On an iPhone or iPad, Safari deletes a website's saved data if you use Safari for a week without visiting it. A game added to your Home Screen is not affected, so add Endless Labyrinth to your Home Screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and play it from there.

Differences from the Windows version

  • On a phone or tablet, gestures instead of keys, and a Gesture Practice in place of the Key Test. Phones have no T key, so in driving, and in chaos's battles, roads, tower floors and islands, the double tap's report also gives the seconds left on the clock.
  • During a run the game speaks with Bella, with the room's echo; the menus, Game Over and your final score use your device's own voice, with a Speech Rate setting on the main menu. The Windows version speaks with the Windows voice throughout.
  • On a phone, tablet, Chromebook, Mac or Linux computer there is no Braille output, since the screen reader is off while you play. On a Windows computer the game works (from web 3.0.0) with a Braille display and a Braille keyboard through NVDA or JAWS, as the Windows version does: it keeps the keyboard focus in a game area of its own and shows everything it says there on the display, so a Braille keyboard's letters reach the game at once, a key held down on a Braille notetaker counts as one press, contracted wordsigns typed as text are turned back into the letters they stand for, and the main menu has a Speech setting, to turn the game's own voice off, and a Braille Key Test.
  • High scores are kept separately from the Windows version's.
  • Ultimate Mode, Find the Voice, The Abyss, The Edge, Water Park, The Museum, Toy Memory, Volcanic Caves and Story Mode exist only here, and so do their levels in Chaos Mode. The website is heard in binaural 3D, through your browser's own 3D sound, and its walking modes turn with you; the Windows version is in stereo, with north always ahead and the four arrows stepping north, south, west and east.
