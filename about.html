About and how to play

Endless Labyrinth, the web version.

What the game is

Endless Labyrinth is a 3D audio game: you play it by ear, with headphones. It began as a game for Windows computers. Since November 2026 the complete game also plays in a web browser, on a phone, a tablet or a computer: all six modes and the events of the Windows version, and nothing to install. Web version 2.0.0 adds a seventh mode made for the web alone, Ultimate Mode; see below. Since web version 5.0.0 the whole web game is heard in binaural 3D sound and its walking modes turn with you, which the Windows version, in stereo, does not do; the rules of each mode are otherwise kept as close to the Windows version as the two can be, and both play the same events.

Want to know how the game came to be? The Story of the Game tells how it grew from a single labyrinth found by ear into six modes, events, a Windows release and this web version, and how I make it: designing and testing by ear, with Claude writing the code.

Supported devices

Phones and tablets, played with touch gestures: an iPhone or iPad with iOS 18.4 or newer, or an Android phone or tablet with an up-to-date Chrome.

Computers, played with the keyboard: a Chromebook, a Mac or a Linux computer. Chrome, Edge or Safari is recommended; Firefox should work, but the game is not tested with it. On a Mac, Safari needs macOS with Safari 18.4 or newer.

Windows computers play the whole game on the website too, from web version 5.0.0, with the keyboard. Endless Labyrinth also has a full Windows version to download, which works offline, with Braille display support and its own room echo on the voice: the Windows version.

How the sound works

Everything is placed by sound. From web version 5.0.0 the whole game is heard in binaural 3D, the browser's own head-related panning, so with headphones a sound is placed properly in front of you, behind you or to one side, not only left and right. Distance is loudness: the closer something is, the louder it gets. Something behind you also sounds duller, as if heard over your shoulder. In Standard, Battle, Tower, Island, the walking levels of Chaos and Ultimate Mode the world turns with you: you turn where you stand, every sound sweeps round you as you do, the background included, and you walk the way you are facing. Driving, a road in Chaos, the events and an elevator ride do not turn: there north is always ahead. In the labyrinth, the battle arena, a tower and a road's tunnels, every sound has a slight echo of the place it is in, shaped by the walls around you and by the air of each level; islands, the seabed and the open road have none. In Ultimate Mode every area of the world has an echo sized to that area, and a sound can be below you as well. Together these make a space you can move through by ear.

Starting

  1. Put on headphones and open the play page. On an iPhone or iPad, turn silent mode off: it mutes the game's sounds, though not its voice.
  2. Press Start. The first time, the game downloads its sounds, about 120 megabytes, so use Wi-Fi if you can; after that they stay on your device.
  3. The game speaks for itself, so it asks you to turn your screen reader off. On an iPhone or iPad, triple-click the side button (or the Home button) if your Accessibility Shortcut is set up, or ask Siri. On Android, hold both volume keys for three seconds if that shortcut is set up. On a Mac, press Command F5 for VoiceOver; on a Chromebook, Control Alt Z for ChromeVox; on Linux, Super Alt S for Orca.
  4. With the screen reader off, swipe up anywhere on the screen to begin, or on a computer press Enter.
  5. When you leave the game, turn your screen reader back on the same way.

Gestures

Swipe up or down
In a menu, move through it. In a run, one step the way you face, or one step straight back. In an event, where you do not turn, one step north or south.
Flick left or right
A quick turn of 30 degrees, in every mode that turns: Standard, Battle, Tower, Island, the walking levels of Chaos and Ultimate Mode. Three flicks take you from one main direction (north, east, south or west) to the next. In an event it is one step west or east, and in driving a lane change.
Slide left or right and keep your finger down
In every mode that turns: you keep turning for as long as your finger stays on the screen, moving or resting, and stop exactly where you lift. A finger that lifts at once was a flick; one that stays down is a slide.
Slide up and hold
Run, during a run: you step forward at once, then keep stepping, fast (280 steps a minute), for as long as your finger stays on the screen.
Slide down and hold
Walk backwards, during a run: you step back at once, then keep stepping, more slowly (200 steps a minute), for as long as your finger stays on the screen.
Double tap
Choose, like Enter. During a run, the report: in standard mode your clock, and in the other modes everything that mode keeps track of, such as your ammo, kills and wave in battle. In driving, and in chaos's battles, roads, tower floors and islands, it also gives the seconds left on the clock; in chaos it ends with your points.
Two-finger tap
Attack, like the spacebar: the laser, a throw or a shot down the road.
Three-finger tap
Pause, and resume.
Triple tap
Back, in menus and while paused. While paused it asks whether to quit the run.
Four taps
Help, on the main menu and while paused.
Hold a finger still for a second
In Ultimate Mode only, the compass, on or off. In every other mode a finger resting still does nothing.
One tap
Nothing.

Only lifting your finger stops a run or a backward walk; you also stop by yourself at a wall, at the edge of an arena, and whenever a new level begins. On a touch screen you cannot turn while you run: lift your finger, turn, then run again. Lift it, too, before you shoot with two fingers or double tap. Running and walking backwards work in every mode and in events, except driving, which has its own accelerator, and an event where up and down are answers rather than steps.

Driving

Driving, in driving mode and on a chaos road, works its own way. Put a finger on the screen and slide it up: the car accelerates for as long as that finger stays on the screen. Lift it and the car coasts. Swipe down to brake: each swipe is one brake, and if your accelerating finger slides back down without lifting, it stops accelerating and brakes once. To change lanes, lift your finger and swipe left or right. To shoot or hear the report, lift first, then tap with two fingers or double tap; the car coasts meanwhile.

During a run only the double tap is counted, so it acts at once and an extra tap can never end a run by accident. The main menu has a Gesture Practice that says the name of every gesture it feels. A Bluetooth keyboard also works, with the Windows game's keys: the arrows, Enter, Space, Escape, P, H and T, and C for the compass.

Every topic from the game's own help, with a Play button for every sound, is on the Help page.

Keys, on a computer

On a Chromebook, Mac, Linux or Windows computer, the game uses the Windows game's keys, and everything it says names the keys instead of gestures. In the modes that turn, the left and right arrows turn you, a press for a quick turn of 30 degrees and a held arrow for as long as you hold it, and the up and down arrows step forward and back, or run and walk back while held. Gesture Practice is not on the menu.

Arrow keys
Move one step, or move through a menu. In driving, hold the up arrow to accelerate, press the down arrow to brake, and left and right to change lanes. In Ultimate Mode the left and right arrows turn you, a press for a quick turn of 30 degrees and a hold to turn freely, and the up and down arrows step the way you face and straight back; held down, they run and walk backwards, and you can turn while you run.
Enter
Choose. During a run, the report, as the double tap above.
Space
Attack: the laser, a throw or a shot down the road.
P
Pause, and resume.
Escape
Back, in menus. During a run, it asks whether to quit the run.
H
Help, on the main menu and while paused.
T
The time left on the clock.
C
The compass, in Ultimate Mode.

The game pauses by itself if you lock your device, switch apps or leave the page.

On an iPhone or iPad, the game's sounds dip while its voice speaks, and come back about a second after. That is how iOS mixes speech with other sound, and a web page cannot change it.

Standard mode

Find the exit of each labyrinth before the clock runs out. You start with 45 seconds, and every level you complete adds 7 seconds. The exit is marked by a beep that rises and quickens as you get closer. Walls give a sharp knock and you do not move. Time credits, marked by a spinning beacon, turn up in about a third of levels. Your score is the level you reach.

Battle mode

No walls and no exit: endless waves of enemies come at you. Whatever sounds centred between your ears is what your shot takes, in front of you or behind. Between waves comes a break, the only time ammo and time credits are laid out, one at a time, for as long as the break lasts; run dry in the middle of a wave and a single emergency cartridge appears, once a wave. The clock starts at 60 seconds and never stops. Your score is the number of enemies you shoot down.

Driving mode

A two minute run down an open road in one of three lanes. Time credits on the road buy more clock, which is the only thing that ends the run. A crash costs thirty seconds and all of your speed. Two high scores: cars destroyed, and distance in metres.

Tower mode

Climb a tower one floor at a time. A rival guards each floor and races you for the items lying on it, then throws them at you; the stairs appear once you have destroyed it. Every tower is three to ten floors, and each one is one and a half times as hard as the last. There is no clock. Your score is the floors you climb.

Island mode

A chain of islands, and the only way across is down. Clear each island of enemies, ride its elevator to the seabed, find the key while your air lasts, and ride up to the next island. Nothing is described to you: every island sounds different, and everything that happens has its own sound.

Chaos mode

Every mode at once, and the hardest in the game. Each level is chosen at random from a labyrinth, a battle, a tower floor, a stretch of road or an island, and nothing tells you which: you work it out from what you hear. Every run starts with exactly five minutes on one clock, and time credits add more; your score is points.

Ultimate Mode

The hardest mode in the game, made for the web alone (web 2.0.0) and the first that was never part of the Windows game. Chaos tests switching between modes against the clock; this tests survival. One huge, continuous world is built at random every run from five to ten areas of five kinds, each keeping its home mode's sounds: walled labyrinths, islands with a shore, sand and an elevator down to the seabed, tower areas with throwers racing you for items, battle areas with chasers, and safe zones, which sound like the places of the Windows game's events and have no enemies. There is no clock: you start with 100 health and the run ends when it reaches zero, with no hit ever taking more than 25. At a random moment, anywhere from one minute to four into each world and with no warning but a five-second countdown, the whole world regenerates at random, and you must work out where you are again by ear; your ammo, tower items and health carry over, and everything you earn is worth more with every world you survive. Since version 4.0 the world turns with you: you turn where you stand, by quick turns of 30 degrees or a held turn that stops exactly where you let go, with a soft tick at each main direction and a higher one for north; the backgrounds turn round you with everything else; you walk exactly where you face, sliding silently along a wall you meet at a slant; and a shot takes the nearest enemy within 30 degrees either side of straight ahead. A compass, turned on by holding a finger still for a second (C on a keyboard), muffles the world and leads you along the walkable route to the nearest safe zone: its centred beep rises as you turn to face the way to go, and it names each direction you face and describes what lies that way. Since version 4.1 a water slide drops you into a pool you have to wade or swim out of, and here and there stands a humming glass case: smash it with your attack and what was inside is yours at once, but two to five fast chasers hear the glass break and come for you, to be shot or outrun. The whole mode is heard in binaural 3D, with every sound placed around you and each area ringing with its own echo. It is also the one mode a Windows computer can play on this website.

Events

Time-limited challenges, published for the Windows version and played here too, the very same events. When one is running it appears in the Events menu, with its own high scores. Each event is checked as genuine before it can be played.

The game's help, opened with four taps on the main menu, explains every rule of every mode and has demo sections that play every sound. Ultimate Mode has the most detailed section of all, with every area, the enemies, health, ammo, the credits and multiplier, the regeneration, the compass and the 3D sound.

Your scores

High scores are kept on your device, in your browser, and are never sent anywhere. On an iPhone or iPad, Safari deletes a website's saved data if you use Safari for a week without visiting it. A game added to your Home Screen is not affected, so add Endless Labyrinth to your Home Screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and play it from there.

Differences from the Windows version

  • On a phone or tablet, gestures instead of keys, and a Gesture Practice in place of the Key Test. Phones have no T key, so in driving, and in chaos's battles, roads, tower floors and islands, the double tap's report also gives the seconds left on the clock.
  • The voice is your device's own, with a Speech Rate setting on the main menu. It has no room echo.
  • On a phone, tablet, Chromebook, Mac or Linux computer there is no Braille output, since the screen reader is off while you play. On a Windows computer the game works (from web 3.0.0) with a Braille display and a Braille keyboard through NVDA or JAWS, as the Windows version does: it keeps the keyboard focus in a game area of its own and shows everything it says there on the display, so a Braille keyboard's letters reach the game at once, a key held down on a Braille notetaker counts as one press, contracted wordsigns typed as text are turned back into the letters they stand for, and the main menu has a Speech setting, to turn the game's own voice off, and a Braille Key Test.
  • High scores are kept separately from the Windows version's.
  • Ultimate Mode exists only here. The whole web game is heard in binaural 3D and its walking modes turn with you; the Windows version is in stereo, with north always ahead.
