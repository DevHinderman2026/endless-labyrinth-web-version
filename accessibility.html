Accessibility

Display options, and how to set up your device and screen reader for the game.

Endless Labyrinth is made to be played entirely by ear. This page has two things: display options for anyone who can see some of the screen, and a setup guide for each kind of device. How the site itself is built for screen readers is in the Accessibility Statement.

Display options

For low-vision players. These change every page of this site and the game's own screen, on this device, and they are kept with your other settings (a backup carries them).

Setting up your device

Two things are the same everywhere: wear headphones, because left and right matter, and turn your screen reader off while you play, because the game speaks for itself with your device's own voice, then turn it back on when you leave. The game asks for this at the start of every session and tells you the shortcut.

iPhone and iPad

  1. VoiceOver reads this website. To turn it on or off at any time, triple-click the side button (the Home button on older models) if VoiceOver is in your Accessibility Shortcut, or ask Siri to turn VoiceOver on or off.
  2. Turn silent mode off (the switch or button on the side), or the game's sounds will be muted while its voice still speaks.
  3. Add the game to your Home Screen and play from there: in Safari, open the Share menu and choose Add to Home Screen. Safari deletes a website's saved data after a week without visiting it, but a game on the Home Screen keeps its scores.
  4. Open the game, press Start, and when the sounds have loaded, turn VoiceOver off and swipe up anywhere to begin.
  5. When you leave the game, turn VoiceOver back on the same way.

Android phones and tablets

  1. TalkBack reads this website. To turn it on or off at any time, hold both volume keys for three seconds if that shortcut is set up (Settings, Accessibility, TalkBack), or ask your assistant to turn TalkBack on or off.
  2. Use an up-to-date Chrome. Turn the media volume up; the game's sounds play through the media volume.
  3. To add the game to your Home screen, open Chrome's menu and choose Add to Home screen or Install app.
  4. Open the game, press Start, and when the sounds have loaded, turn TalkBack off and swipe up anywhere to begin.
  5. When you leave the game, turn TalkBack back on the same way.

Chromebook

  1. ChromeVox reads this website. Control Alt Z turns it on or off at any time.
  2. The game is played with the keyboard: the arrow keys, Enter, Space, P to pause, Escape to quit and H for help. Every key is listed in About and how to play.
  3. Open the game, press Start, and when the sounds have loaded, turn ChromeVox off with Control Alt Z and press Enter to begin.
  4. When you leave the game, turn ChromeVox back on with Control Alt Z.

Mac

  1. VoiceOver reads this website. Command F5 turns it on or off at any time.
  2. Use Safari 18.4 or newer, or Chrome. The game is played with the keyboard; every key is listed in About and how to play.
  3. Open the game, press Start, and when the sounds have loaded, turn VoiceOver off with Command F5 and press Enter to begin.
  4. When you leave the game, turn VoiceOver back on with Command F5.

Linux

  1. Orca reads this website. Super Alt S turns it on or off on most desktops.
  2. Use Chrome or Chromium if you can; Firefox should work but is not tested. The game is played with the keyboard; every key is listed in About and how to play.
  3. Open the game, press Start, and when the sounds have loaded, turn Orca off and press Enter to begin.
  4. When you leave the game, turn Orca back on the same way.

Windows

This website reads with NVDA, JAWS or Narrator like any other. From web version 5.0.0 the whole game plays here on Windows, with the keyboard, and with a Braille display and Braille keyboard through NVDA or JAWS. There is also the full Windows version to download, from the Releases page of the game's GitHub repository: it works offline, with NVDA and JAWS, with a Braille display, and with its own room echo on the voice.

  1. Open the game, press Start, and when the sounds have loaded, quiet your screen reader's speech: with NVDA, press the NVDA key plus S, which cycles the speech mode off and back on; with JAWS, press Insert plus Space, then S (Caps Lock plus Space, then S, in the laptop layout), which switches between full speech and speech on demand. Then press Enter to begin.
  2. When you leave the game, turn the speech back on the same way.

With a Braille display or a Braille notetaker such as the BrailleNote Evolve: the game keeps the keyboard focus in a game area of its own from the moment the sounds are loaded, so NVDA or JAWS stays in focus mode, every key reaches the game, and everything the game says appears on your display and stays until the next message. It is not a text box, so the letters you braille reach the game at once, with no need to press the space, and a key held down on a notetaker counts as one press. Contracted wordsigns typed as text, such as have for H, are turned back into the keys they stand for. Your screen reader would also speak each message, so turn its speech off while you play and let the game speak; or turn the game's own voice off with the Speech setting on the main menu and keep your screen reader's. The main menu also has a Braille Key Test that names every key or word that arrives; try it first with a new device.
