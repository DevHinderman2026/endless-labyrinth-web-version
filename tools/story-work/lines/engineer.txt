~The storm's my favorite weather. It's loud, it's bright, and it powers my workshop for free.
The world is a machine, walker! A huge one! And every few minutes, it resets itself, like a machine that's been switched off and on again.
I call the rebuild the Reboot. Everything shuts down, the gears spin back to the start, and up it all comes again. Clean and new.
There's a great engine somewhere under the world. I can hear it through the factory floor. Rumble, rumble, clank. It never stops.
The lightning strikes five times right before every countdown. Five bolts, five booms. Then the rebuild. I've measured it. It's very precise.
I think the lightning powers the rebuild. All that energy has to come from somewhere. Where does it go? Into the new world, I reckon.
The machines in the factory build parts I don't recognize. Pipes, walls, little bits of labyrinth. I think they're building pieces of the next world.
Hazel thinks the world is magic. Magic is just engineering that hasn't been explained yet. I've told her. She turned my wrench into a fish. The fish is fine, by the way. I keep it in a bucket.
Pendle thinks the world is a clock. A clock is a machine, so he's half right. Cinder thinks it's a forge. A forge is a workshop, so he's a quarter right.
I tried to build a machine that would stop the rebuild. It worked for one second. Then the world rebuilt anyway, and my machine became a toaster.
The storm clouds always gather over the middle of the world before a rebuild. Like the world is charging up. Then, boom. New world.
I opened a panel in the factory wall once. Behind it, more gears. Behind those, more gears. I kept opening panels for a whole world. Gears all the way down.
The machines in the factory never break. I've never once repaired them. Something fixes them between worlds. Something very good at its job.
I found a blueprint in the factory. Very old, very faded. It's a plan of the world. But it's missing one piece, right in the middle. Just a blank square.
Some say the Builder is an engineer, like me. Builds a world, tests it, finds a problem, and starts over. That's how I work, too.
If I were the Builder, I'd want feedback. So, if you're listening, Builder, the labyrinths are a bit confusing, and the enemies throw too much.
The rain in the storm is warm. That's not normal. Warm rain means something underneath is very hot. Probably the great engine. Probably.
The world's mercy is a power surge. I'm sure of it. A little extra energy, sent through the world, out of nowhere. Somebody's generous with the electricity.
The enemies don't like my factory. Too noisy, I think. Or they're scared of the conveyor belt. Can't blame them. It's very fast.
I've heard a voice in the static of my radio. Just before a rebuild. It says, recalibrating. Then it goes quiet. I've never found the broadcast.
The factory has a door I can't open. Heavy, metal, with a warning sign. The sign is blank. Who makes a blank warning sign? What's it warning about?
Every world, the factory makes one thing I didn't ask for. Last world, it was a key. This world, it was a tiny bell. Next world, who knows.
The factory keeps making bells and keys. I think they're parts of something. If I could collect all of them, I could build it. I just don't know what it is.
Thunder is just the sound of the air moving out of the way of the lightning. Maybe the rebuild is the world moving out of the way of something.
I measured the world once, end to end, with a very long tape measure. Next world, I measured again. Same size, to the inch. Different shape. How?
The steam pipes whistle a tune right before the countdown. Five notes. Everybody's heard that tune somewhere. I want to know who wrote it.
I tried recording the rebuild with a machine I built. When I played it back, all I heard was a click. One click. Like a switch. Who flips it? My best guess so far is, someone with a very big finger.
The lightning in the storm always strikes the same spot. A tall iron pole in the middle of nowhere. I didn't put it there. Nobody did. It's just always there.
There's a rumor among the engineers that the world is a test run. And one day, the real world will be built. When the test is finished. I'd love to see the real thing. It must be enormous. With better plumbing.
The factory has a control room with a thousand buttons. None of them are labeled. I've pressed most of them. Nothing happens. Except one. One button makes the lights flicker. I've not pressed that one again.
Captain Brine says something under the sea keeps count. I bet it's a machine. A counter, ticking up. One more world. One more world.
Mister Pendle and I argued for a whole world about whether the world is a clock or an engine. Then it rebuilt, and we had to start over. We'll get there.
The factory floor is warm, always. Put your hand flat on it, and you'll feel it humming. The world's motor, running and running.
Every machine needs fuel. So what does the world run on? I've got a theory. Curiosity. Ours. It runs on us asking why. Maybe that's why it never stops. Nobody's stopped asking.
The storm never passes. It just circles the factory, round and round, like it's guarding it. Or like it's waiting for me to finish something.
I built a compass once that points to the great engine under the world. It points straight down. Every world. Straight down. Very helpful.
The conveyor belt sometimes carries things from the old world into the new one. A cup, a shoe, a spring. Then they drop off the end, and I never find them again.
Some engineers say the world rebuilds because it's overheating. Too many walkers, too many enemies, too much going on. It needs to cool down and restart.
Maybe the world isn't overheating at all. Maybe it's warming up, getting ready for something big. That's what I'd do, if I were a world.
Lady Rime won't let me put a thermometer in her ice cave. She says some things should stay unmeasured. I say everything should be measured. We're both very stubborn.
Every answer I find in this factory comes with a new question attached, like a gear with another gear behind it. Turn one, and the next one spins.
~Safety first, walker! Well, curiosity first. Then safety. Then lunch.
~I keep a lightning bottle on my desk. It's empty now. The lightning got out. It's somewhere in the factory. Don't worry about it.
The rivets in the factory walls count up. One, two, three, all the way around. The last rivet is always missing. Every world.
Every machine in the factory hums a little louder right before the rebuild. Like they're all saying, here we go again, together.
I built a listening machine to hear the edge of the world. It picked up one sound. Someone, very far away, tightening a bolt.
The thunder here rolls in fives. Boom, boom, boom, boom, boom. Then silence. Then the rebuild. I've got a chart. It's very convincing.
There's a lever in the factory labeled, do not pull. I haven't pulled it. Somebody has. Every world, it's in a slightly different position.
The factory chimney puffs smoke rings. Perfect rings, one for every world. They float toward the middle of the world and disappear.
I tried to take a gear from the old world into the new one. I held it tight. In the new world, my hand was holding a flower. A metal flower.
The storm's lightning writes letters in the sky. Just for a flash. I've seen an A|letter, an N, and something like a question mark.
Every invention I make, the world copies. I made a tiny fan last world. This world, there's a giant fan in the middle of the labyrinth.
The bus drivers out there drive like machines. Never a wrong turn, never a pause. I'd love to look under their hoods. Not that they have hoods.
I think the world was built to be fixed. Over and over. Like a machine made by someone who loves fixing things more than finishing them.
There's an old engineer's rhyme. Wind|verb it up and let it go, where it stops, nobody knows. I always thought it was about toys. Now I'm not so sure.
The world's gears are too big to see. But you can feel them turn. Stand very still in any safe place, and you'll feel the tiny shudder of a gear moving on.
My radio once picked up a broadcast from a world long gone. A weather report. It said, sunny, with a chance of rebuilding. Ha. Somebody has a sense of humor.
The labyrinth walls are made of a material I can't identify. Not stone, not metal, not wood. Warm, hard, and very slightly springy. Like it's grown, not built.
Sometimes the storm stops just long enough for me to hear the world's engine clearly. It's running smoothly. Somebody is very good at maintenance.
I tried to build my own world in a box. A little one. It worked for five seconds. Then it rebuilt itself into a slightly better box.
The factory whistle blows once per world. Right at the rebuild. End of shift. New shift begins. Somebody keeps very strict hours.
I think the Builder is an inventor, and the world is their best invention. And every inventor knows, there's always one more improvement.
I keep a notebook of every machine I've ever found in the factory. Ninety nine machines. I'm sure there's a hundredth. It's just hiding.
The lightning rod on the roof glows faintly between worlds. In the dark, quiet in between. Like a lighthouse, helping the next world find its way.
Somebody oils the gears between worlds. I can smell the fresh oil every time. Somebody's taking care of this place. Taking care of all of us.
