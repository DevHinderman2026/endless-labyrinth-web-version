~I'm Marigold. I sell a bit of everything, and I hear a bit of everything too.
~Apples, ribbons, candles, rumours. Rumours are my best sellers.
~Mind the chickens, dear. They think they own the place.
~I've had this stall since before the first world. Or so it feels. My feet certainly think so.
~The baker next door burns everything. Every single world. It's a gift, really.
~Haggling is half the fun, love. The other half is gossip.
~I always know what the weather's doing. My knees tell me.
~If you're buying, I'm selling. If you're not, I'm still chatting.
~The village bell rings every hour. Nobody knows who rings it. Nobody minds, either.
~You've a kind face, love. Well, a kind set of footsteps. That's what I go by.
Everyone who comes to my stall has a different story about why the world rebuilds. I write them all down. I've got a cart full now.
The best rumour I ever heard? That the world rebuilds because somebody, somewhere, keeps losing their keys and turning everything over to find them.
The village restocks itself every world. Every apple, every candle. I never order a thing. I'd love to meet the supplier.
Credits are a funny currency, love. No face on them, no mint mark. I've never been able to work out who's making them.
The market is busiest just before the countdown. Everyone hurrying, everyone buying. As if they know something's coming.
I sold a map once to a walker. A map of the world. She came back the next world and said it was perfect. For the last world.
Old Tobias at the tavern swears the streets rearrange themselves at night. Well, of course they do. The whole world does.
There's a stall at the far end of the market that's always closed. Shutters down. I've never seen it open. But I've heard someone inside, counting.
A traveller told me the world is a great market, and we're all the goods on the shelves. Being shuffled about. I didn't care for that one.
My grandmother had this stall before me. She said the world used|hab to stay the same for days. Days! Can you imagine?
The village has a well in the middle. Drop a coin in, and you never hear it land. Some say it lands in the next world.
I've noticed the cobblestones change pattern every world. But the path to my stall is always the same length. Funny, isn't it?
They say the Builder visits the market in disguise. Buys one apple. Pays with a coin from a world that hasn't happened yet.
Mister Pendle the clockmaker says there's a timetable for the rebuilds. If there is, he won't sell me a copy.
The chickens always run in circles before a rebuild. Three times round the cart, then they hide. I've learned to pack up when they start.
I once tried to carry my stall into the next world. Packed it all on my back. Woke up in the new world, stall set up, everything in place. Back still aching.
Some folk think the enemies out there are stealing pieces of the world. I've never had a thing go missing. Except my good scissors. Who knows.
Every world, I find one thing on my stall I never put there. Last world it was a little silver bell. This world, a feather.
The village has a crier, but he only ever shouts one thing. All is well! All is well! Then the countdown starts. I don't think he's paying attention.
There's a rumour going round that the safe places are the world's pockets. Somewhere to keep the important bits safe while it shakes itself out.
I heard from a pedlar who heard from a sailor who heard from a fish, that something at the bottom of the sea is keeping count of the worlds.
They say a walker once bought the whole market, every last thing. Next world, it was all back. And the walker? Never heard of again. Probably still carrying it.
Hazel the witch buys herbs from me. She never says what they're for. Last time, she bought every bit of thyme I had. Thyme, love. Time.
You know what I think? I think the world rebuilds because it's restocking. Running low on something. And it hasn't found it yet.
Some walkers save up all their credits for something special. What, I always ask? They never know. The world never says what credits are really for.
The village gate opens onto the world every time, but it never opens onto the same world twice. I've stopped expecting the same view.
I keep a ledger. Every world, the totals come out exactly the same. Not near enough. Exactly. To the last penny. What does that tell you?
The baker swears his bread rises in time with the world. When the bread's done, the world's done, he says. His bread is always done.
I asked a traveller where he'd come from. He said, the edge. I asked what was there. He said, a fence, and a sign. He wouldn't tell me what the sign said.
There's a song the children sing in the village square. Round and round the world goes, where it stops, nobody knows. Sweet, isn't it? A bit unsettling, now I think about it.
They say there's a coin with a face on it, the only one. Whoever finds it will know who made the world. I've checked every coin I've ever had.
The village is older than the labyrinths. I'm sure of it. The labyrinths were built around us, like a fence round a garden.
I've noticed the walkers sound a little different each world. More sure of themselves. Maybe the world is teaching you something, love.
When the world shows its mercy, the whole market goes quiet. Even the chickens. Like everyone's listening to the same good news.
The market sells everything except answers. Believe me, I've tried to stock them. They go stale far too quickly.
A man came by once and paid for an apple with a key. An old iron key. I've still got it. Nobody in the village has a lock it fits.
Some say the world rebuilds because it's untidy. I say, have you seen my stall? And the world doesn't rebuild that.
I used|hab to think the countdown voice belonged to the village crier. Then I heard them both at once.
The bell in the church tower rings a different number of times every world. I've started writing the numbers down. They're not random. They're not anything I know, either.
The traders all have their own name for the rebuild. The Restock. The Shuffle. The Big Sweep. I call it closing time.
A pedlar told me there are other markets, in other worlds, all happening at once. And every one has a stall like mine, with a woman like me in it. Gives me a funny feeling.
Every world, someone leaves a basket of eggs at the edge of the village. No farm, no hens nearby. Just eggs. Fresh ones.
The village well whispers sometimes. Mostly about water. Once, it said a name. Your name, maybe. It was hard to hear.
They say the first world had only one shop, and it sold only one thing. A single question. Nobody could afford it.
Some folk believe the world rebuilds whenever someone tells a lie. Well, the market would be rebuilding every second, love.
The weathervane on the inn points the same way every world, no matter which way the wind blows. Toward the middle of everything.
I gave a gift to a walker once, and they gave me one back. A smooth stone, warm, humming. It's on the shelf. It goes quiet just before each rebuild.
I once overheard two travellers arguing about the Builder. One said they build the world for the walkers. The other said, then why do they keep knocking it down?
Captain Brine buys rope from me every world. A great length of it. I asked what for. He said, to tie the world down. It hasn't worked yet.
You know the funny thing, love? Everyone's got a theory, and no one's got the answer. Best business there is, rumours.
~My apples are the crunchiest in any world. I'd stake my stall on it.
~Mind the goose by the well. She bites. Only walkers she likes, mind you. So she might bite you.
The market bell rings five times before the countdown. Then all the traders shout, last orders! Nobody knows why we do that. We just always have.
There's a fortune teller at the end of the lane. She never tells fortunes. She just says, ask me in the next world. Then the next world comes, and she says it again.
I keep a little box of things walkers have traded me. Buttons, string, a whistle that plays one note. Every world, the whistle plays a different note.
The cobbler in the village says every walker's footsteps sound a little different. Yours, love, sound like someone who's going to find something.
They say the market was the first place ever built, and the world grew around it like a town around a crossroads. Every road leads here, eventually.
The world's mercy sweeps through the market like a cool breeze on a hot day. Everyone stops and smiles. Then back to haggling.
The scales on my stall weigh things true, every world. Except once. They weighed nothing, and it came out heavy. I still can't work out what was on them.
There's a rumour the Builder trades in worlds. One world for another. Somewhere, there's a market where whole worlds are bought and sold.
The candlemaker's candles always burn for exactly one world. Light one at the start, and it goes out right at the countdown. He doesn't know how.
I've stopped trying to work out why the world rebuilds, love. I just make sure my stall is ready when it does. A good trader is always ready.
A little girl once gave me a drawing of the market. In it, every stall was empty, except mine. And in mine was someone I didn't know, smiling.
Every world, there's one coin in my till that's warm. Just one. Like someone was holding it a long time before they paid.
The tinker who sells pots says he's sold the same pot to Chef Rosalind a hundred times. She never remembers buying it. The pot remembers, he says.
The village has a statue in the square. Nobody knows who it's of. Every world, the statue faces a different direction. Always toward the walker.
