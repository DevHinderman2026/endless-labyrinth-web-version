~I'm Captain Brine. Been sailing these waters longer than the world's been changing.
~Hear that creak? That's my old ship. She talks more than I do.
~The sea's calm tonight. It's always tonight, out here. But it's calm.
~Every captain needs a good ship, a good crew, and a good song. I've got the ship, anyway.
~Mind the barnacles on the rail. They're older than you. Older than me, nearly.
~I'll tell you a sea story or two. All true. Mostly.
~Never trust a calm sea, sailor. And never trust a seagull. They steal biscuits.
~The ocean's the biggest safe place there is, if you know how to float.
~Salt in the air, wind in the sails. Best way to live, in any world.
~My crew went ashore three worlds ago. Haven't come back. I'm sure they're having a lovely time.
The sea is the only thing that doesn't change, sailor. Every world rebuilds, but the tide comes in and goes out the same. Or so I tell myself.
Something at the bottom of the sea is keeping count of the worlds. I've heard it. Every rebuild, a deep knock. One more. One more.
I call the rebuild the Tide. It comes in, washes everything away, and leaves a brand new beach behind.
My charts change every world. I draw them fresh, and the next world, the coast is somewhere else. I keep drawing anyway. Habit.
There's a lighthouse I've never been able to sail to. I can hear its horn. Every world, it's the same distance away.
Down on the seabed, there's a door. Not a hatch, not a hole. A door, in a frame, standing on the sand. I've never had the breath to swim down and knock.
The islands out there rise and sink with every world. Some say they're the backs of great sleeping creatures. Gentle ones. Just resting.
Hazel says the sea tastes less salty every world. I say she's got her spoons mixed up. Then again, my soup has been a bit bland.
The ship creaks five times before the countdown. Five long creaks. Like an old sailor counting to himself.
I've sailed to the edge of the world twice. The first time, there was fog. The second time, there was more ocean. Neither time, an answer.
The sea gives back what it takes, they say. I dropped my compass overboard three worlds ago. Next world, it washed up on deck. Pointing the other way.
Underwater, the world sounds different. Closer. As if you're inside it, listening to its heartbeat. And it's always beating a bit fast.
Willa on the river says all water flows to the sea. Then where does the sea flow to, eh? Somewhere it goes. It drains away, between worlds.
Old sailors say the world is a ship, and the rebuild is the ship being turned about. Same ship, new heading. Somebody's at the wheel. I've never met them. But I've felt the deck lean, just before a rebuild, as if someone took a hard turn.
The bubbles that rise from the deep, just before the countdown, all pop in the same order. Pop, pop, pop, pop, pop. Then here we go.
The fish know before anybody. They all swim one way, all at once, just before the rebuild. Toward the deep. I'd like to know what they're swimming to.
I found a bottle floating once, with a note inside. It said, the world is fine, keep sailing. No signature. Same handwriting as my own logbook.
There's a rumor among sailors that there's a sea underneath the sea. Darker, quieter, older. And the world floats on it, like a cork.
The world's mercy comes in on the wind, sailor. You feel it on your face. A warm breeze from nowhere. Then the sails fill, and you've a little more time.
I keep the ship's bell polished. I don't ring it. But once in a while, between worlds, it rings by itself. Just once. Like an answer to a question I haven't asked.
The enemies don't swim. Not one. I've watched. They stop at the water's edge every time. Something about the sea keeps them out.
The seabed is covered in old things. A teacup, a bell, a wheel, a shoe. Things from worlds that are gone. The sea keeps them, like a scrapbook.
Lady Rime says the great clock that runs the world is under the ice. I say it's under the sea. We've argued it for forty worlds. Neither of us has looked.
They say a whale sings the song that starts each world. I've heard a long, deep song under the waves. Whether it's a whale or something else, I couldn't tell you.
The stars at sea are different every world. I navigate by them anyway. They always lead me back to the same place. Here.
The deeper you dive, the quieter the world gets. Until, at the very bottom, there's nothing but a hum. I think that's the world thinking.
Every captain has a ghost story. Mine's this. One night, I heard my own ship creaking, far off in the fog. But I was standing on her deck.
The tide went out once and didn't come back for a whole world. The sea floor was bare. And right in the middle, carved in the sand, a circle. And in the circle, a question mark.
I've got a theory, sailor. The world rebuilds because something under the sea turns over in its sleep. One day, it'll wake up. Then we'll know. Or maybe it's awake already, and listening, same as you.
Pendle swears the world runs on a clock. Well, the sea runs on the moon. And the moon, as far as I can tell, runs on nothing at all.
I once tried to anchor the ship so she'd stay put through a rebuild. Dropped the heaviest anchor I had. Next world, the anchor was gone, and the rope was tied in a bow.
The sea air carries voices sometimes. Snatches of songs, old words. I think it's carrying them from worlds long gone, the way it carries salt.
There's a lantern that floats out on the water at night. No boat under it. Just a lantern, drifting. I've followed it. It goes round in a great slow circle.
Fennick in the marsh says the frogs know everything. Frogs! Fish know more than frogs, sailor. Fish just don't brag about it.
I've sailed through a thousand worlds, and every one smells of the sea. Every one. Maybe the sea is the oldest thing. Maybe the world grows out of it.
The ship's figurehead is a woman holding a lantern. Every world, the lantern is turned a little more toward the deep. I've never moved it.
The creaking of my ship is a language, sailor. I've learned most of it. Just before a rebuild, she always says the same word. I won't tell you which. It's private.
Divers come back from the seabed with strange stories. A light down there that follows you. A voice that says, almost. I've heard that one many times.
The waves come in sets of five. Five waves, then calm. Then the countdown. I've counted them for a thousand worlds.
If you want my honest opinion, sailor, nobody knows a thing. But the sea knows something. And the sea's not telling.
~Every sailor's got a lucky charm. Mine's a button. Don't ask what it's off. It's a long story.
~Mind the hatch, sailor. Fell down it in my first world. Never again. Well, twice again.
~The galley's got biscuits. Hard as rocks, but they float. Very useful at sea.
The sea glows at night, just before a rebuild. A soft green light in the waves. Then it goes dark, and there's a new sea.
The tide comes in five times faster just before the countdown. Rushes up the beach. Then the world changes, and the beach is somewhere else.
There's a ship that sails past every world. Far off, in the fog. Same sails, same creak. Never answers when I hail her. I think she's my ship. From another world.
The deep water is the oldest water. It's been down there since before the first world. If you could taste it, you'd taste the beginning of everything.
Every captain's log has a last page. Mine never does. I keep writing, and there's always another page. The book grows with the worlds.
I once caught a fish that sang. Just a little tune, three notes. I let it go. It seemed important. Next world, I heard the whole sea singing those three notes.
Old sailors say the world is an island, floating on the great sea of everything. And the rebuild is the island turning, to show a new side.
The seagulls know when the countdown's coming. They all fly up at once, screeching. Then the world changes, and they settle on new rocks.
The islands out there have elevators down to the seabed. Who built them? Not sailors. Sailors use ropes. Somebody very clever, and very tidy.
There's a sunken ship on the seabed, with my ship's name on it. Same name, older paint. I've never had the nerve to dive down and read the log.
The world's mercy, to a sailor, is a fair wind. You can't call it. You can't make it come. You just have to be ready when it does.
Barnacles grow on the hull every world. I scrape them off. Every world, they grow back in the same pattern. Like writing. I can't read barnacle.
The ocean at night is the quietest place I know. Just the creak of the ship and the slap of the waves. Perfect for thinking about impossible things.
I asked the sea why the world keeps changing. A wave splashed over the rail and soaked my boots. I'll take that as, mind your own business.
The anchor chain rattles by itself, right before every countdown. Five rattles. My ship knows what's coming.
Sailors tell a tale of a captain who sailed into the space between worlds. She came back with a bag of stars and no memory of how she got them.
Some say the enemies came from the sea, long ago, and they've been trying to find their way home ever since. That's why they're always moving.
The lighthouse horn sounds once before every rebuild. Low and long. Like it's warning ships away from something. Or calling them toward it.
The ship has a cabin I never go in. The door's always locked. Sometimes, I hear someone in there, turning the pages of a map.
There's a current in the sea that always flows toward the middle of the world. Drop anything in, and it'll float there, eventually. Nothing ever comes back.
A sailor's job is to keep going, whatever the weather. Same as yours, walker. Keep going. The answers come to those who keep sailing.
