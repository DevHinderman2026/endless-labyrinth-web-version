~Forgive the volume. The organ only knows one way to speak, and that's grandly.
~The choir rehearses every evening. They're very dedicated. I've never seen them leave.

The world is a piece of music, walker. A great, long piece. And every few minutes, it returns to the opening bars.
There is one pipe in this organ I've never been able to play. The longest one. When I press its key, nothing sounds. But the whole world trembles. I believe that pipe plays the note that starts each world.
The choir sings the same hymn before every rebuild. It has no words, only a melody. Every world, the melody is a little longer.
I once played a chord just as the world rebuilt. The chord carried on into the next world. I took my hands off the keys, and still it played.
The great organ sometimes plays itself between worlds. I have heard it from the next world, faintly, like a memory.
Hazel thinks the world is a spell. Jangles thinks it's a song. I think we're both nearly right, which is to say, both quite wrong.
The rebuild sounds, to my ear, like a great chord resolving. Tension, then release. Then, oddly, more tension.
I have a theory. The world is waiting to hear one perfect chord. When it hears it, it will stop rebuilding. I've been trying for a very long time.
There's sheet music in the organ loft in a hand I don't recognise. It's labelled, for the last world. The pages are blank.
The countdown voice is perfectly in tune, did you know? Every number lands on the same note. Somebody musical is counting.
Some evenings the dark choir sings a name. Very softly. I can never quite catch it. They say they don't know whose it is either.
The echo in this cathedral is longer than it should be. Much longer. As if the sound is travelling somewhere far before it comes back.
I believe each safe place has its own note. The kitchen hums in one key, the river in another. Put them all together, and you'd hear the world's chord.
The organ bellows breathe in and out, like lungs. Just before a rebuild, they breathe in, and hold. Five beats. Then everything changes.
The world's mercy, to me, sounds like a single sustained note, played very gently. As if somebody leaned on a key by accident. A kind accident.
There is a hymn book here with one hymn missing. The page has been torn out cleanly. I've looked in every world for that page.
The choir master told me, long ago, that the first world was a single note, held by a single voice. And then the voice ran out of breath.
I've noticed the cathedral is a little larger every world. A new pew, a new pillar. Someone is building it slowly, between the rebuilds.
Prudence in the library says the world is a book. Music and books are close|adj cousins. Both are read|past from the start. Both can be played again.
The bells in the tower ring once before each rebuild. I don't ring them. The ropes hang perfectly still. And yet, they ring.
I believe the enemies out there are out of tune. Just slightly. They throw things because they can't sing.
The dark choir hums lowest when walkers are near. I asked them why. They said, so the walker can hear the floor of the world.
There is a pedal on this organ, the lowest of all, that I never press. My teacher told me never to. I've wondered why for a hundred worlds.
Some say the cathedral was here before the labyrinths. That the labyrinths were built to keep its music in. Or to keep something else out.
I tried once to play the countdown on the organ. Five notes, falling. When I played the fifth, the world rebuilt. I haven't played it since.
The world is a round, I think. You know, a song sung over and over, each voice starting a little behind the last. We're all singing it.
The stained glass windows hum when the sun is low. There's no sun here, of course. And yet, every world, they hum.
I asked the oldest singer in the choir what the hymn is about. She said, it's about coming home. Whose home, I asked. She smiled and sang on.
Every rebuild, the organ goes slightly out of tune. Every world, I tune it again. Perhaps the world is doing the same thing, with itself.
Music fills a room and then fades. The world fills with life and then rebuilds. I find a great comfort in that. The room is never truly empty.
The ghost choir in the graveyard and the dark choir here sing the same hymn, a few bars apart. Neither choir has met the other.
There is a rumour that a song is hidden in the shape of every world, if you walk it from end to end. I believe it. I just don't have the legs for it.
I once heard a voice join the choir that wasn't one of ours. Clear and bright. It sang one line, then fell silent. The choir still talks of it.
I believe the Builder is a composer. Writes a world, listens to it, isn't satisfied, and begins a new draft. Every great composer does the same. But then, who is the audience? Who is the Builder writing it for?
The organ's lowest notes travel through the floor. Put your hand on the stone, and you'll feel them. And underneath them, faintly, another organ, playing back.
Every world, I play the same piece at the same moment. Every world, the cathedral answers with a slightly different echo. The world is never the same listener twice.
There's a little door behind the organ pipes. It's too small for me. Sometimes, from behind it, I hear someone tuning.
The rebuild never interrupts the music. Have you noticed? It always waits for the end of a phrase. Somebody very polite is in charge.
The choir believes they are singing the world into being. That if they stopped, it would all fall silent. I've never asked them to stop.
I have played for walkers, for ghosts, and once, I believe, for the Builder. A figure sat in the back pew for one whole world. When it ended, the pew was empty, and warm.
A note played in this cathedral takes eleven seconds to fade. Except just before a rebuild. Then it takes five.
The world is unfinished music, walker. And unfinished music is the most beautiful kind. You can always imagine the next note.
~The organ bench is rather hard. I've sat on it so long, I've stopped noticing.
~I play quietly in the mornings. Well, as quietly as an organ can. Which is still rather loud.

The organ has five keyboards. Four of them I play. The fifth plays itself, very softly, during the countdown.
The choir's robes are never worn out, never new. Exactly the same, every world. As if they're made of the same quiet thread.
I believe the cathedral is the world's ear. It's shaped to listen. Every sound in the world eventually reaches here, and echoes.
The organ's pipes are cleaned between worlds. Spotless, every time. I've never seen who does it. But I hear them humming as they work.
There's a hymn the choir sings only once per world. The last line is always different. Last world it was, and so we wait. This world, and so we wake.
The world's mercy, in the cathedral, is heard as a single bell, ringing gently. Not the tower bells. A smaller one. Somewhere above the ceiling.
The dark choir sings in the lowest voices in the world. Lower than any bell. So low, I believe, that the world itself is listening to them.
I once played a scale that went down and down, below the lowest note. The organ kept playing. Lower and lower. Then the floor began to hum. I stopped.
I've a feeling the countdown is not the end of a piece, walker. It's the turning of the page. The music carries on, on the next page.
Prudence once found a hymn book in the library written in music only. No words. When I played it, the choir began to sing words none of us knew.
There's a stone in the cathedral floor marked with a single musical note. If you stand on it, you can hear the whole building humming that note.
I believe the enemies outside would be calmer if they could hear the organ. Music soothes. I'd play for them, if they'd only come in.
The echoes in the cathedral sometimes come back changed. You play one note, and a different one returns. As if the walls are correcting you.
Some say the cathedral was built around a sound, not a place. A single sound, held so long it became stone. I find that rather beautiful.
The choir stalls have names carved in them. Every world, there's one more. The newest name is always a walker's. Perhaps yours, one day.
Every organist before me kept a list of the world's tunes. My list is the longest. And every tune on it ends the same way. Unresolved.
The organ's lowest pedal note is not a sound. It's a feeling. You feel it in your chest. Just before the rebuild, it plays itself, very softly.
Jangles came to play his fiddle in the cathedral once. Fiddle and organ together. The choir wept. Then the world rebuilt. We never finished the piece.
I believe there's a final piece of music, somewhere, for when the world finally rests. I hope I'm the one who gets to play it.
Every world, I play the first note just after the rebuild. I like to think it helps the new world find its voice.
