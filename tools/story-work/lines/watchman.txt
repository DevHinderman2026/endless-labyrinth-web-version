~I'm Tobias. I keep the night watch. Somebody has to, and the night's always here.
~Have a seat, friend. The cocoa's hot and the stew is better than it smells.
~I walk the same streets every night. They're never the same streets. It keeps the job interesting.
~My lantern's been lit since before the first world. Never needed new oil. Don't ask me how.
~Mind the cat by the door. She belongs to nobody and everybody.
~The tavern's warm, the streets are cold, and I'm somewhere in between. Always have been.
~I've got a little book where I write down everything odd I see at night. It's a very thick book.
~All quiet out there, mostly. The trick is knowing which quiet is the good kind.
~Old habits. I check every door twice. Even in a world that'll be gone in a few minutes.
~Pull up a stool, walker. I'm not much for talking, but I'm good at listening.
I've walked these streets every night for longer than I can say, and I'll tell you this. They rearrange themselves. Every single world.
Every world, the city has one more street. I count them. I've never found where the new one goes.
I keep a logbook of the rebuilds. Time, weather, anything odd. There's always something odd. Never the same odd thing twice.
The lamps go out one by one just before the countdown. Not all at once. One by one. As if someone's walking down the street, blowing them out.
Folk in the tavern argue about the rebuild every night. The Builder, the dream, the spell. I just listen and pour the cocoa.
I've seen a figure at the end of my street, just before a rebuild. Tall, still, holding something. By the time I got close|adj, the world had changed. I'm sure it meant no harm. It was waiting, I think, like the rest of us.
The clock in the square stops at the same time every night. Then the world rebuilds, and it starts again. Pendle won't say why.
There's a door at the end of Lantern Lane that's painted a different colour every world. I knocked once. The door knocked back, very politely.
A walker in the tavern told me the city is the oldest part of the world. Built first, and everything else grew out from it. I like that idea.
On quiet nights, I hear a sound under the cobbles. Like gears turning. Like the whole city is sitting on a very large clock.
Marigold at the market says the world rebuilds to restock. Well, the tavern's never short of anything. So what's it restocking?
I found a letter once, under the tavern door. It said, keep watching. Nothing else. No name. It was in my own handwriting. I don't remember writing it.
I think the watchman's job is the same as the walker's. Keep your ears open. Notice what's changed. And never stop asking why.
The city sounds different the moment after a rebuild. Like a room just after someone's left it. The air's still moving.
The cat by the door always knows when a rebuild's coming. She stretches, yawns, and goes under the table. Every time.
They say the city was once a single street. One street, one lamp, one watchman. I've a feeling that watchman was me.
Some nights I hear trains from the station, but there are no tracks out that way. Mister Pendle says those are trains from another world, passing close|adj.
The enemies never come into the tavern. Never. I've seen them stop at the doorstep and turn around. Something keeps them out.
I asked the oldest regular in the tavern why the world rebuilds. She finished her cocoa, set down the cup, and said, it's practising. Then she left.
The rain out there always falls from the same direction. From the middle of the world, outward. Like something's breathing out.
There's a rumour among the watchmen that the city is a puzzle, and when the right walker solves it, the world will stop rebuilding.
I don't know if I want the world to stop rebuilding. It's the one thing I can count on.
If you walk to the end of every street in the city, you come back to the tavern. Every street. Every time. I've tried.
The world's mercy came through the city once while I was on watch. Every lamp burned a little brighter. Then back to normal. Lovely, it was.
I wrote down every rumour I heard in the tavern for a whole world. Forty three rumours. Not one of them agreed with another.
The fog rolls in from the river just before a rebuild. Captain Brine says it's the sea's breath. Willa says it's the river's. I say it's damp, and leave it at that.
There's an empty chair in the corner of the tavern no one sits in. Every world, there's a cup of cocoa on the table in front of it. Still warm.
The bells in the city ring at odd hours. Once, they rang five times, slow, and the countdown started right after. Coincidence, I tell myself.
A traveller told me there's a city in every world, and a watchman in every city. I asked if they all look like me. She just smiled.
Some say the city was built by the Builder as a place to rest. Some say the Builder lives here. I've checked every house. Not yet found them in.
I've noticed something on my rounds. The city is always laid out a bit like a labyrinth. The labyrinths out there are always laid out a bit like a city.
On the stroke of every rebuild, the tavern sign swings, though there's no wind. As if somebody's just walked in.
The cobblestones are warm at night, even in winter. Something underneath is keeping them warm. Or something underneath is awake.
I used|hab to think the countdown came from the clock tower. Then I stood in the tower and heard it coming from outside.
The night never ends here. I've been on watch so long, I've forgotten what morning sounds like. Wren says it sounds like birds. I'll take her word for it.
I once followed a stranger through the streets for an hour. Every corner, they were just ahead. At the last corner, they were gone. Just a lantern, left lit.
In the tavern, they tell a story about the first walker. She asked the world a question, and the world has been rebuilding ever since, trying to answer.
Some in the tavern say the first walker gave the world an answer, and the world didn't like it. I prefer the story where she asked a question.
Every world, the tavern has a new song painted over the fireplace. The words are always nearly the same. One word changes. I've been keeping track.
The street names change every world. Lantern Lane, Quiet Row, Five Bell Street. Last world, there was one called Why. I didn't walk down it.
The sound of the city at night is the best map I know. Shutters, footsteps, a kettle somewhere. When the sounds go strange, I know a rebuild's near.
Hazel came into the tavern once and ordered a cup of nothing. I poured it. She drank it. She said it tasted of the world before this one.
A walker told me she heard her own footsteps behind her, out in the city. When she stopped, they kept going. Then they turned a corner and were gone.
I think the city remembers you. Doors you've walked past open a little easier. Lamps you've passed burn a little longer.
The night watch has one rule. Whatever happens, keep the lantern lit. Nobody told me why. I've a feeling it matters.
They say if every lamp in the city goes out at once, the world won't come back. I've never let that happen. I'm not about to find out.
Sprocket says the city runs on gears. Hazel says magic. The cat says nothing. Of the three, I trust the cat most.
I hear the tavern's old piano play the same tune every night at closing. The tune ends right as the countdown begins. Every time.
Somewhere in this city, they say, is a window that looks out onto the space between worlds. I've looked through a thousand windows. Not that one, yet.
~Mind the puddle by the door. It's been there since the first world. I've grown fond of it.
~The fire in the tavern never goes out. I keep it fed. It keeps me warm. Good partnership.
The tavern's old clock chimes once per world. Always at the same moment. Just before the countdown. Then it stops, and waits for the next world.
I found a map of the city scratched into the bottom of a tavern table. Every world, the map is different. And it's always right.
The bus drivers stop by the tavern sometimes. They never sit. They never drink. They just stand by the door, listening. Then they go.
There's a street in the city where your footsteps echo twice. Once for you, and once for someone just behind you. I've never seen who.
The world's mercy sometimes comes to the tavern as laughter. Out of nowhere. Everyone starts laughing, and nobody knows why. Then the world has a little more time.
The cobblestones are laid in a spiral, if you follow them. Round and round, all the way to the middle of the city. Then the spiral starts again.
I've noticed the enemies avoid lamp light. Every one. They never step into the glow. I keep the lamps lit for the walkers' sake.
There's a rumour that the night watch was started by the first walker. To keep the lamps lit, so the next walker could find their way.
A walker once told me the city is just one big safe place, and the labyrinths are its garden walls. I rather liked that.
I keep a list of every face I've seen in the tavern. Different faces, every world. But one seat is always taken by someone who never says a word.
The city fountain runs backward just before a rebuild. Water going up instead of down. Then it stops, and the new world starts the fountain fresh.
The stars over the city are always arranged the same. A wide circle, with one bright star in the middle. Like a clock face, with no hands.
Every lamp post in the city has a number. I've counted them. The last number is always one higher than the world before. The city's keeping count.
I've worn the same coat on my rounds for a thousand worlds. It's never worn out. It's got a pocket I never use. Every world, there's a note inside. It says, thank you.
The tavern keeper before me said the world will stop rebuilding when every walker has had a warm meal and a kind word. I'm doing my part.
