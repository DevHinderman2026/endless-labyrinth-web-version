I keep a record of every world I've seen. The record is very long, and every entry ends in the middle of a sentence.

I'm sure the world rebuilds for a reason. Everything happens for a reason, in a well written story. I just haven't reached that chapter.
Every world, there's one new book on the shelves that I didn't put there. Always the same author. No name, just a little drawing of a door.

The cathedral's records go back further than the world itself. There are entries for worlds before the first one. I can't explain that.
Ambrose believes the world is music. Music is lovely, of course, but you can't look things up in it.
There's a card catalogue drawer marked, answers. I've opened it a hundred times. It's always empty. But someone keeps relabelling it.
I found a book that was all footnotes. No story, just footnotes. Each one explained a different reason why the world rebuilds. None agreed.

The world rebuilds when the story stalls, they say. When nothing new can happen, it starts over. So perhaps it's waiting for something new.
Every world, the library has one more shelf. I can never find where it was added. It's simply there, full of books.
There's a reading room here that is always locked. I have the key. The key doesn't fit. Every world, I try anyway.
The records say the first walker kept a diary. One page has been found, in a dozen worlds. It always says the same thing. Still listening.
I have a theory. The world isn't being rebuilt. It's being read|past again. Each time, by a slightly different reader. You, perhaps.
The candles in the cathedral flicker in the pattern of a sentence, I'm convinced. I've been taking it down for many worlds. So far, it reads, and then.
Mister Pendle wanted to borrow the book on time. I lent it. Next world, it was back on the shelf, unread, with a train ticket inside.
The enemies out there are like misprints, I think. Errors in the text. The world keeps trying to correct them.
There's a map in the cathedral archive that shows every world at once, layered on top of each other. It's mostly ink. Very dark, very old ink.
I'm told there's a library in every world, and it's always mine. But the books in it change. As though the world can't decide what I should read.
The world's mercy is like finding an extra page in a book you thought you'd finished. A little more story. Unexpected, and very welcome.
In the oldest book in the cathedral, there's a word that appears on every page. I can read every word in the book except that one.
Some scholars believe the countdown is a reader closing the book. Five, four, three, two, one, and the cover shuts. Then it opens again. A different book.
I file every rumour I hear under R. The R drawer won't close any more.
The books here are bound in a leather that's warm to the touch. Every world, they're a little warmer. As if they're being read|past somewhere else, at the same time.
There's a rule in this library. Never read the book on the bottom shelf, in the corner. I wrote that rule. I don't remember writing it, or why.
I've noticed something in the records. The worlds are getting longer. Only by a moment or two. But longer. As if the world is learning to be patient.
The cathedral has a register of everyone who has ever walked in. Your name is in it, many times. Some of the entries are dated in the future.
A walker once asked me for a book on how it all ends. I looked for a whole world. When the countdown came, I found it. It was a book on how it all begins.
They say the Builder wrote a guide to the world, and left it here. It's the one book I've never found. Perhaps they're still writing it.
The ink in my pen runs dry precisely at each countdown. Every world, it's full again. Someone thinks my notes are important.
Every library has a quiet. Mine has two. One is ordinary. The other is the quiet between worlds, which seeps in through the walls, very slightly.
I keep a book of contradictions. Hazel says this, Mortimer says that. It's my thickest volume. Every rumour has its opposite, filed beside it.
Prudence is a family name. The librarians before me were all called Prudence. I've seen the records. There have been a great many of us.
Some believe the world rebuilds because it was written in pencil. Easy to rub out, easy to write again.
The pages of the book of worlds are not numbered. I tried to number them once. When I came back, the numbers were all five.
I've found letters tucked inside books, written to no one, signed by no one. They all ask the same question. Have you found it yet?
The cathedral archive has a room with nothing in it but a reading stand and a single book, closed. No one is allowed to open it. No one remembers who said so. I opened it once, just a crack. The book was reading me, very politely. I closed it again.
There's an index at the back of the oldest book. Under W, for world, it says, see also, again. Under A|letter, for again, it says, see also, world.
I believe every story told in every safe place is true, in its own world. The trouble is, we only ever get to live in one of them at a time.
When the world rebuilds, I always lose my place in whatever I'm reading. I've started using a bookmark. The bookmark always stays. The book changes.
In the cathedral, there's an inscription above the door, too worn to read. I've traced it with my fingers a thousand times. I think the first word is welcome.

I believe the world is not a single book, but a library. Every world is a different book. And you, walker, have been reading them all.
