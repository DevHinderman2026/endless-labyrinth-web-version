Update History

What each version of the web version of Endless Labyrinth brought.

This page lists the web version. For the Windows version, see its changelog on GitHub.

The game updates by itself: the next time you open it, you have the latest version. Only a sound pack that has changed is downloaded again, and your high scores are kept. Events are not listed here: they appear in the Events menu as they open.

5.0.0 (12 November 2026)

The whole web game is now heard in binaural 3D sound, and its walking modes turn with you. Until now only Ultimate Mode did either. Standard, Battle, Tower, Island and the walking levels of Chaos now turn exactly as Ultimate Mode does, and several of their numbers have changed to allow for it. Ultimate Mode's glass cases and seabed have grown. Windows computers can now play the whole game on the website. And the events gain everything Windows version 3.0.0 brought: building blocks for rooms, creatures and goals, and thirty-two new kinds of mission.

The Windows version and this web version are developed side by side. Compatibility between them, the events included, will be maintained to the highest extent that both can allow. The Windows version stays in stereo, with north always ahead.

New: binaural 3D sound in every mode

  • Every enemy, beacon, item, credit, wall and explosion is now placed by the browser's own 3D sound, in front of you, behind you or to one side, as Ultimate Mode's sounds have been since 2.0.0. Wear headphones.
  • This is in every mode: Standard, Battle, Driving, Tower, Island, Chaos and the events. Distance is still loudness, and a thing behind you still sounds duller as well.

New: the walking modes turn with you

  • Which modes: Standard, Battle, Tower, Island and the walking levels of Chaos, as well as Ultimate Mode. You turn where you stand, the whole world turns round you, the background included, and you walk the way you face.
  • Which do not: Driving, a road in Chaos, an elevator ride and all the events. There north is always ahead and the controls are as they were. The events stay that way so that they play the same on the website and in the Windows version.
  • On a touch screen: flick left or right for a quick turn of 30 degrees. Slide left or right and keep your finger down to keep turning; lift to stop exactly there. Swipe up to step the way you face, and down to step straight back. Slide up and hold to run, as before.
  • On a keyboard: press the left or right arrow for a quick turn, or hold it down to keep turning. Press the up or down arrow to step, or hold it to run or walk backwards. On a Braille notetaker, each press is one quick turn or one step.
  • A flick and a slide are now told apart by one thing only: how soon your finger lifts. A finger that lifts at once is a flick. A finger that stays down is a slide, whether it keeps moving or rests. Until now a slide that kept moving could be taken for a swipe, so turns sometimes went missing or were too small to hear. A held turn also begins faster than it did. This applies to Ultimate Mode too.
  • Ticks: as you turn, a soft tick marks east, south and west, a higher tick marks north, and a quick turn that lands between two of them gives a softer click.
  • The report: a double tap, or Enter, now ends with the way you are facing, such as Facing north-east.
  • Walls and edges: walked straight into, a wall or an arena's edge stops you with its sound, as before. Met at a slant, you slide along it in silence.
  • Aiming: in Battle, on a tower floor and on an island, a shot or a throw takes what is centred straight ahead of you or directly behind you, by the way you face. Enemies in Battle and on islands now come from any side.

Changed: Standard Mode

  • You start with 60 seconds (it was 45).
  • Time credits are worth 20, 30 or 40 seconds (they were 15, 20 and 30).

Changed: Battle Mode

  • You start with 80 seconds (it was 60).
  • Enemies come in turns, never all at once. In waves 1 to 3 one at a time: the next appears only after you shoot the one before. In waves 4 to 6 two may be after you at once, in waves 7 to 9 three, and from wave 10 four, which is the most there will ever be (it was up to eight).
  • During a wave the clock waits whenever no enemy is after you, and runs again the moment the next one appears. During a wave break it runs as it always did.
  • The first wave has six enemies, and no wave has fewer.
  • Time credits are worth 15, 25 or 35 seconds (they were 10, 15 and 20); the 35 is the rarest. Each wave break has between three and six of them, and once you have taken them no more come until the next break.
  • Wave breaks begin at 52 seconds (it was 45) and shrink by a second a wave to 25 (it was 30).

Changed: Tower, Island and Chaos

  • Tower: the rival is a fifth slower, since you now turn to find the item.
  • Island: every dive now has an air vent on the seabed, and ten seconds after you take one, another appears somewhere else. Until now about half of all dives had one. Your air is still thirty seconds.
  • Chaos: its walking levels have all of the above. In its battles, how many enemies may be after you at once grows with the battles you have played in that run: one in the first three, then two, then three, then four. Its five minutes and its credits are unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • Glass cases knock. The ping added in 4.1.1 is replaced by a knock: two quick raps on glass, from where the case stands, within fifteen steps of it, faster and louder as you close. Turn until the knock is centred between your ears. The crystal hum is still there underneath and leads you in from further off.
  • The bell now means you are lined up: beside the case and facing it. Then your attack smashes it.
  • Time to line up: within three steps of a case that is not yet broken, no enemy can hit you, for up to fifteen seconds in all at that case.
  • A case's time reward is 60 seconds (it was 30).
  • A bigger seabed, as big as Island Mode's: twelve steps from the elevator to its edge, with two or three cartridges on it.
  • Treasure chests. About one seabed in three has a chest, far from the elevator. Swim onto it and it opens: two or three things at once out of shots, points, health, a bundle of tower items and, rarely, ninety more seconds for the world above. The voice says only what was inside.
  • The one-time notice about turning, said at the first run since 4.0.0, is gone. Nothing is announced inside the game any more; this page says it instead.

Changed: Windows computers

  • A Windows computer now plays every mode and the events on the website, with the keyboard, and with a Braille display and Braille keyboard through NVDA or JAWS. Until now it played Ultimate Mode only. The Windows version remains available to download.

New: events from Windows version 3.0.0

  • Events can now be built from general building blocks: a main floor with patches of a second one, walled rooms, glass and display cases, up to three kinds of creature, a listening creature that wakes when it hears you, traps, safe spots, teleports and chests, a meter such as air or warmth, levels, stages, and spoken story lines.
  • Thirty-two new kinds of mission, among them finding memories by their musical fragments, smashing cases against a clock, crossing glass floors without waking anything, and reaching shelter before a storm.
  • A creature that listens growls when it wakes, then charges, and you hear its footsteps running at you, from where it is and on whatever floor it is crossing.
  • An event played in levels can begin with few creatures and add more each level, up to a most the event sets. Trapped in Darkness, for example, begins with one creature and never has more than three.
  • Broken glass from a smashed case covers only the case's square and the squares around it, and a listening creature is never placed on top of an item or right beside one.
  • When an event gives its items several sounds, each item out at once takes a different one, so you can tell them apart by ear.
  • An event can give you unlimited shots: the laser never runs out, and you hear "unlimited shots" in place of a number.
  • An event made with any of these needs web version 5.0.0 or Windows version 3.0.0. Events made the earlier way play as before.

The website

  • The Help page's frequently asked questions have six new questions about turning.
  • For thirty days this page opens with an alert that says what has changed in the controls. It is a region named Alert at the top of the page, like the home page's, and it never interrupts what you are reading.
  • The home page, About, Help, the Accessibility pages and the Terms describe the game as it now is.

4.1.2 (11 November 2026), web only

Two corrections to Ultimate Mode after playing 4.1.1. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • No wall sound in a corridor you are walking along. Since a swipe became a turn of 30 degrees, you are often facing a corridor a little off its line. Each step then grazed the corridor's side wall, so you heard a footstep and a wall at the same time on every step, as if you kept walking into walls. Now a step that slides along a wall makes no wall sound: you hear a wall only when it actually stops you. And when you are facing mostly along the corridor, each step takes you a whole step down it, not a shorter one. Facing well off its line, the steps along the wall are shorter, as before, and still silent. The same goes for an island's edge and the edge of the seabed.
  • The voice says only the reward. When you smash a glass case, the voice now says just what you got, for example "8 shots", without "Glass case" in front of it.

4.1.1 (11 November 2026), web only

One addition to Ultimate Mode's glass cases, after their first play test: the hum alone was not enough to centre a case. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • A glass case pings. Within twelve steps of a case, in its own area, a short, glassy ping now repeats from where the case stands, on top of its hum. Its pitch tells you how squarely you face the case: highest when the case is straight ahead, one step lower for every 15 degrees it is off to either side, and lowest when it is behind you. Turn until the ping is at its highest, then walk. It repeats faster the closer you come. The game makes this sound itself. The help's Glass Cases topic explains it, and there is a new demo, Glass Case Ping.

4.1.0 (11 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode's turning, tuned after its first play test on a phone, and two new things to find in its worlds: a pool to climb out of at the foot of every water slide, and glass cases to smash, with a reward inside and a price to pay. A swipe turned you too far to bring anything to the centre, the aim was too forgiving, the backgrounds did not turn with the rest of the world, and the compass said too little. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

New: Ultimate Mode

  • The pool. A water slide used to put you down on dry ground somewhere in the park. Now you splash into the middle of a pool, in another part of the park, and have to get out of it yourself: whichever way you go, three steps are in the water and the fourth is on dry ground. A step in the pool is a wading step. If you run through it, you swim, and each step is a swimming stroke. Step back from the edge and you are in the water again; the pool stays where it is until the world regenerates. The steps are cut from Walking in the water 3 by shakil10000, and the strokes from Swimming Sounds by DRAGON-STUDIO, both from Pixabay.
  • Glass cases. Here and there in a world, in battle areas, on islands and in labyrinth areas, stands a case made of glass: at most three in a world, and some worlds have none. Never in a safe zone, and never in a tower area. A case hums where it stands (the crystal hum from the Windows game's events), and the compass names it among the things nearby. Come within a step of it and a clear bell chimes once. Then your attack, a tap with two fingers or the spacebar, smashes the case instead of firing, and costs no shot. What was inside is yours at once, with nothing to pick up, and the voice says what it was: points, sixty times your multiplier; shots; thirty more seconds in this world; thirty health, if you are below full; or two or three tower items.
  • The price of breaking glass. The enemies hear it. Between two and five chasers set out for you from the far side of that area: usually two or three, four now and then, five if you are very unlucky. The voice does not say how many, and at first you cannot hear them; but they are three times as fast as the area's own chasers at the start of a run, and faster later, though never as fast as your run. If your reward was shots, it is always one to three more shots than the chasers that are coming. Shoot them, and each pays as any chaser does and none comes back in its place; or run, because they hunt only in the area where the glass broke, and once you have left it, on foot, by bus or by an island's elevator, they give up and are gone. In a labyrinth area they follow the corridors, as you must.
  • Broken glass. The glass lies on the floor where the case stood, two steps round it, and crunches under your feet until the world regenerates. The smash is Glass Shatter by Universfield and the steps are cut from Footsteps on glass shards by OxidVideos, both from Pixabay.
  • Help. A new topic, Ultimate Glass Cases, and the pool in Ultimate Water Parks; and six new demos: wading, swimming, the glass case, its chime, the smash, and a step on broken glass. The Credits page lists the four new recordings.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • A swipe turns you 30 degrees, not a quarter turn. Three swipes left or right, or three presses of an arrow key, take you from one main direction to the next, and six turn you right round. A thing you hear can now be brought to within 15 degrees of the centre with swipes alone, which makes walking straight to a cartridge, a credit, a potion or a tower item much easier. Landing on north, east, south or west still gives its tick, with the higher tick for north; landing between two of them gives a softer, lower click, so every swipe is heard. Sliding and holding to turn freely is as it was.
  • The aim is a little less forgiving. A shot or a throw takes the nearest enemy within 30 degrees either side of straight ahead; it was 45. An enemy within two steps of you is still hit wherever it is. Quick turns alone can still bring any enemy in front of you.
  • The backgrounds turn with you. Standing inside an area, its background recording used to stay put while every other sound swept round you. Now the recording's left side stands in the west of the world and its right side in the east, so when you turn it turns round your head with everything else; facing north it sounds as it always did. A battle area's drums and its crowd, a safe zone's background and the water round you on the seabed do the same. A few recordings are the same on both sides, and those sound the same whichever way you face.
  • The compass describes. It no longer says only "This way". Every turn with the compass on begins with the way you now face. Facing the way to go, it says so, describes what lies that way and gives the walk, for example: "North, the way to the safe zone. Open for 6 steps, then a wall. Beyond, a tower area, 9 steps away. Walk 6 steps this way, then turn left, to face west." Facing any other main direction it describes that way and ends with where the way to go is, by name: "The way to the safe zone is north, to your left." Between two main directions it says the way you face and where the way to go is in degrees: "Facing north-north-east. The way to the safe zone is north, 30 degrees to your left." The briefing names directions too: "The safe zone is 40 steps away, starting east, to your right."
  • The report names the way you face with one of sixteen words, such as north-north-east, so that each step of a quick turn has its own.

4.0.0 (11 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode turns with you. Until now north was always ahead, and a swipe both faced you and stepped you. Now you turn where you stand and the whole world sweeps round you, you walk exactly where you face, and you aim by facing your enemy. It is the biggest change the mode has had. Every other mode is as it was. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • The world turns with you. A swipe left or right is now a turn, not a step: a quick turn to the next main direction, north, east, south or west, swept round in a quarter of a second, so two swipes turn you right round, and if you had stopped in between, a swipe squares you up again. A swipe is a quick turn however fast or slowly you make it, even if your finger pauses for an instant before it lifts. Slide left or right and hold, and you turn freely for as long as you hold: slowly at first, so a short hold is a small nudge for aiming, then faster, up to a full circle in about two and a half seconds. You stop exactly where you lift. Every sound around you sweeps round with the turn, the ones already sounding included; nothing in the world has moved, you have.
  • You walk where you face. A swipe up is one step the way you are facing and a swipe down is one step straight back, at any angle, so you can face a beacon by ear and walk straight to it. Running and walking backwards go the same way. Squarely into a wall you stop, as before. At a slant you slide along it, with the wall heard quietly beside you. Squared up with a quick turn, a labyrinth's corridors work exactly as they did.
  • A tick at each main direction. As you turn, a soft tick marks east, south and west as you pass them or land on them, and a higher tick marks north, so there is always one fixed point to map the world by. The game makes these sounds itself.
  • The aim is the quarter in front of you. A shot or a throw takes the nearest enemy within 45 degrees either side of straight ahead, so one a little to the side still counts, and nothing behind you; a chaser within two steps is still hit wherever it is. Before, a shot took whatever was roughly ahead of you or behind you. Quick turns alone are always enough to bring any enemy in front of you.
  • The compass turns you for real. With the compass on, your turns are your own turns: the beep rises as you face the way to go and falls as you turn away, and it beeps faster while you hold a turn. Each time you land on a main direction it says what lies ahead and where the way is: for example, This way, 6 steps, then left; or, Open for 4 steps, then a wall. The way is to your right. Turn the compass off and you are already facing the way you chose. Before, the compass had a pointer of its own and never turned you.
  • The report says the way you face. It now ends with one of eight words: for example, 100 health. 0 points. 10 shots. 3 items. Facing north-east. The line on the screen says it too.
  • On a computer, the arrows can be held. In Ultimate Mode the left and right arrows turn you, a press for a quick turn and a hold to turn freely, and the up and down arrows step forward and back. Held down, they run and walk backwards, which a keyboard could not do before, and you can turn while you run. A Braille notetaker, which cannot hold a key, makes one quick turn or one step with each press.
  • Smaller things. You step out of an island's elevator with your back to it, so walking on takes you away from it. Stepping anywhere onto the elevator's square starts the ride. A new world leaves you facing as you were, and a new run begins facing north. Gesture Practice names the new slide and hold, and the help topic on sounds behind you says that Ultimate Mode is now the exception to "north is always ahead". And the first time you start Ultimate Mode after this update, the game says once, in a few sentences, that turning has changed; nothing can hurt you while it does, and it is never said again on that device.

Corrected in the help

  • Two sentences that were out of date. The help said that from the next area you still hear a battle area's chasers; you hear an area's enemies only while you are inside it. And the demo of the regeneration countdown mentioned a spoken warning at fifteen seconds, which was removed in 3.1.0: the countdown is the only warning.

3.3.0 (10 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode, made fairer and more dependable: every run begins with a few shots and a few tower items, the items are now yours to keep as ammo is, the report says how many you hold, and in a crowded world the bus's chime can no longer go missing. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • Every run begins with a few shots and a few tower items. Between eight and twelve shots and between two and four items, picked afresh for each run, so you can defend yourself from the first moment, whether you land beside a battle area or a tower area. Before, a run began with neither, and finding some was the first job.
  • Tower items are yours to keep, as ammo is. The items you hold now go with you when you leave a tower area, into the next tower area and into every new world, with no limit on how many you carry. Before, they were gone the moment you stepped out of the area you picked them up in, so every visit to a tower area began with a hunt for one. They are still thrown only in a tower area, where they are still the only weapon; everywhere else the attack fires the laser and the items wait in your hands.
  • The report says your items. The report now gives your health, your points, your shots and your items, in that order: for example, 100 health. 0 points. 10 shots. 3 items. The line on the screen says them too.

Fixed

  • The bus's chime always sounds. In a crowded world, late in a run, the two-note chime that says a bus has come was sometimes cut short or never played at all, and so was the sound of the bus pulling up. The game allows only so many sounds at once, and it was counting every background in the world against that, those of far-off areas you could not hear included: thirty and more of them late in a run. That left almost no room, so in a fight each new footstep or shot pushed out the oldest sound still playing, and the longest sounds went first. Now a short sound can only ever push out another short sound, never a background, and the sounds that tell you something are never the ones to go: the bus pulling up, its chime and its doors, the countdown, the hit and the health warning, a thrower's wind-up, the elevator's ride and its arrival, the water slide.
  • Areas you cannot hear no longer cost anything. The backgrounds of far-off areas used to play on at no volume, all of them, all the time. Now a background nobody can hear is held where it is after a moment and carries on from there as soon as you come within earshot, and the sound of a chaser in an area you have just left stops at the border instead of playing on unheard. Nothing you could hear has changed; late in a run the game simply has far fewer sounds running at once, which is easier on a phone.

3.2.0 (9 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode after more play testing: you step out away from the elevator, tower areas grow hard far more slowly, there are a few more chasers, the aim is kinder, the arena's beacons are louder and ammo no longer piles up. On Windows, a Braille notetaker's keys now reach the game at once. And a new version of the site now shows as soon as it is published. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • Back up from the seabed, you step out a few steps from the elevator. As in Island Mode, the ride up leaves you on the sand four to six steps from the lift, never on it, on a square clear of any pickup and away from the chasers wherever the island allows; before, you came out on the lift itself, one step from going straight back down. The three seconds in which nothing can touch you are unchanged.
  • Tower areas grow hard far more slowly, battle areas hold a few more chasers, and the aim is kinder. A thrower's difficulty now rises one point every two minutes of the run instead of every forty-five seconds, never goes above a middling tower floor, and the hits it takes rise by one every four minutes at most, so ten minutes in it takes about three hits instead of six. Battle and island areas start with one or two chasers instead of one and can hold up to eight instead of six. And a shot or a throw now takes what is roughly in front of you or behind you, a wider aim than battle mode's, and any chaser within two steps of you whatever side it is on, so a close one can no longer be stepped straight past.
  • Beacons carry over the arena, and ammo no longer piles up. Cartridges and credits lying in a battle area now play their beacons twice as loud, so they can be heard over the drums, the drone and the crowd. And every cartridge now holds three to six shots for the whole run: they used to hold more and more the longer you played, on top of there being more of them, so a long run ended with hundreds of shots and nothing to spend them on. There are still more cartridges as the enemies grow more numerous.

Fixed

  • The island elevator hums only on its island. Its chime was already heard only on the island, but its low hum carried into the areas beside it, so you heard an elevator before you had reached the island it stands on. Now the hum, like the chime, begins when you step onto the island.
  • Braille keyboards on Windows: letters reach the game at once. The game kept the keyboard focus in a text box, and in a text box a screen reader holds what you braille until you press the space, so on a BrailleNote Evolve the letter H reached the game only after a space. The game now keeps the focus in a game area that is not a text box: every letter arrives the moment you braille it, and everything the game says still appears on your Braille display and stays there until the next message. The Braille Keyboard setting is gone: a web page cannot see a Braille display, and a notetaker such as the BrailleNote sends its keys just as a keyboard does, so there is nothing to switch on; Braille typed as text by other screen readers is still turned back into the keys it stands for. The Speech setting, which silences the game's own voice, is now always on the main menu.
  • A key held down on a Braille notetaker is one press. Holding one of the BrailleNote's arrow keys walked the player on and on, because the notetaker sends a held key as a quick stream of separate presses. On Windows, presses of the same key that close together now count as one, as a held key does on a keyboard. The Braille Key Test also reports a key held down: how many times it arrived and how far apart.
  • A new version shows at once. After an update was published, a browser could go on showing the old version of the site and the game for up to ten minutes, because it kept its copies of the files that long. Every page now asks for its files by addresses that carry the version number, and a page that turns out to be an old copy opens itself afresh, once, as soon as it is opened (the play page only before you press Start). Your scores, settings and event scores are never touched by it: they are kept apart from the files, and stay exactly as they were.

3.1.0 (9 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode after its sixth play test: healing lives in the safe zones, worlds last a random time and give no warning, the arena crowd rings in the arena, and a run leaves nothing behind when it ends. Web only: the Windows game is unchanged.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • Potions live in the safe zones. While your health is under 75, every safe zone in the world holds one health potion, so whichever safe zone you reach will heal you, and the compass names the nearest in its briefing. Drink one and you are back to 100; the potions in the other zones disappear until you are hurt again, and the zone you drank from has another ten seconds later if you still need it. No potion appears anywhere else any more (it used to turn up anywhere in the world once health fell below fifty).
  • Worlds last a random time, with no warning. Every world is given a length of its own, anywhere from one minute to four, drawn afresh each time; nothing says how long is left, the spoken "World regenerating in 15 seconds" is gone, and the status report keeps quiet too. The only warning is the five-second countdown. Worlds used to last a fixed two minutes.
  • The crowd rings in the arena. Every roar and every reaction of the arena crowd now sends into the battle area's echo, the close-up recordings more than the two cut from a live stadium, which carry the stadium's own; some of them had no echo at all and sounded dry. The stadium crowd in the background is as it was.

Fixed

  • A run leaves nothing behind. When a run of Ultimate Mode ended, its whole last world stayed in memory until the next run began: some thirty sounds with their 3D placement and up to twenty long decoded recordings, the areas' backgrounds. On a phone, three runs of that could be more than the browser would carry. Now everything of a run is released the moment it ends, the 3D sound chain and the echoes with it. A new test plays several games in every mode back to back and watches for anything left over.

3.0.0 (8 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode rebuilt after five play tests in one day: a compass that guides you through the whole world, water parks, buses, an arena crowd, a sound engine that survives a busy regeneration, eighteen new recordings in a new sound pack, and Braille support on Windows. Web only again: the Windows game is unchanged, apart from its credits, which list the recordings the web version now carries. A new major number because so much of 2.0.0's mode has changed: areas, timings, the compass, what a safe zone and a bus do.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • Renamed. The mode released in 2.0.0 as Ultimate Test Mode is now called Ultimate Mode, in the menu, in Help and across this site; this page uses the new name for 2.0.0 too. Its high scores are kept.
  • Help speaks of safe zones throughout, never of event areas, and has five more demos: the diver's breathing, the water slide, the arena crowd's roar, its groan and its gasp, the bus pulling up, its chime and its idling engine.
  • The echo, everywhere. Every area's echo was silent in 2.0.0 (a mistake in how a sound found its area's echo), and even once it rang only placed sounds reached it. Now footsteps, the countdown, the ammo chime, the laser and every other sound ring in the room you stand in, each background rings lightly in its own room, and a safe zone's room is sized to its background: a cathedral, a cave or an ice cave ring long, a kitchen, a library or a tavern are small rooms, the open air has a short light tail. Safe-zone walls ring softer. Islands and the seabed stay open air.
  • The compass is a guide to the whole world, and works in safe zones. Turning it on gives a briefing: where you are, how far the goal is by the walking route and which way to start, and the nearest things worth walking to (ammo, credits, potions, a tower item, the water slide, the elevator), each with its route length and the way to start. The goal is the nearest safe zone, the bus while one waits, or, inside a safe zone, the way out of it. Turned to the way to go it says the leg and its turn ("Right, 6 steps, then ahead", or "Right, 9 steps to the safe zone"); turned anywhere else it says how far the floor is open, what stops you (a wall, the island's edge, the border) and what lies beyond ("Left: open for 4 steps, then a wall. Beyond, a tower area, 9 steps away, then an island"). It used to point only to the safe zone and refuse to work inside one.
  • Bigger, quieter areas. Areas are one and a half times larger, about thirty strides across, a neighbour's background and pickups are heard only within a few strides of its border or doorway, and its enemies only once you are inside. A neighbour's air never stops and restarts as you cross borders. Inside a safe zone the world beyond its borders sounds further off, quieter and dulled as if through walls, and comes back as you step out. On an island, its edge is the boundary buzz of Island Mode, never a wall.
  • No silence between worlds: the next one is there the moment the count reaches zero. Worlds still last two minutes (three and then five were tried in the play tests, and five was too long). A gentler start: one chaser to an area in the first two minutes, slower chasers, two seconds between strikes, and one rival to a tower area.
  • The arena crowd. The stadium crowd has moved from the safe zones to the battle areas, where it roars under the drone and drums, and when a chaser goes down there you hear the bang and then the crowd erupts all round you (seven roars, never the same twice running: two cut from LIVE Football Match – Stadium Crowd Cheering by arunangshubanerjee, Short crowd cheer by driken5482, Crowd cheering by jonasblakewood, Crowd cheering by mykelu, and two swells from Crowd cheering in stadium by vishiv, all on Pixabay). When a chaser strikes you there, you hear the hit and the crowd reacts, six ways: a groan and a gasp (Crowd Disappointment Reaction and Crowd Shocked Reaction by Universfield), a gasp and murmur and a boo (olivia_parker), an aww (adhimahadi) and a boo (DRAGON-STUDIO), all on Pixabay.
  • Tower areas. Only thrown items count there: with no item in hand the attack is the empty click, never the laser, so get more items or leave the area. Items appear between four and ten strides from you, as on a tower floor, instead of anywhere in the area, and the rival's difficulty rises more slowly, its hits never above one more for every two minutes played.
  • Pickups within a stride. A cartridge, a credit, a potion or a tower item is taken from its own square or the one beside it, as Battle, Tower and Island take theirs within three units; the exact square is no longer needed, and the chime comes in the very swipe that lands on it. Cartridges are spread one to an area before any area gets two, and a beacon's echo in a long room is held to the plain room's, so one cartridge never sounds like several.
  • Water parks. A safe zone whose background is water (the ocean, a river, a waterfall, rain, a marsh, the underwater, dripping water) has a water slide, found by Tower Mode's staircase beacon. Step up to it and the ride takes over: you climb the stairs, hear the rush of the water as you slide down (a sound the game makes itself), and splash into the pool, for fifteen points times your multiplier, the one thing a safe zone pays for; the water carries you off to another part of the park, so you find the slide again by ear.
  • Buses. When a safe zone is at least forty-five steps' walk away, a bus may pull up near you (not in every world, never in a world's last minute): you hear it pull in, its air brakes hiss, a two-note chime says it has come, its engine idles where it stands, and the compass points to it while it waits. Step up to it and the doors open, you climb in and sit, the doors close, and it carries you to the nearest safe zone, a quarter of a minute from nearby and up to half a minute from the far end of the world; the doors open, you climb down, they close, and it drives off. Getting on and getting off take about three seconds each. No enemy can reach you on the bus: chasers lose interest, throwers stop throwing and anything in the air falls short; the world outside is quiet and dulled through its windows. Six recordings from Pixabay, credited: Bus at bus stop (derrickmckinnon), Bus Engine Idling and INSIDE BUS (freesound_community), Bus doors open automatically and Bus doors close automatically (spinopel), Bus Leaving From a Bus Stop (freesound_community).
  • The elevator and the seabed, as in Island Mode. Swipes, the shot and the throw are silent in the car; the seabed's edge is the boundary buzz; the way up hums where it stands and your own bubbles come now and then; the ride recording is eased out by arrival and Island's three-second grace holds when you step out. New on the seabed: your own breathing through the regulator, in and out with the bubbles, for as long as you are under (Scuba diving regulator underwater breathing sound effect by ddaedalus, via freesound_community on Pixabay, a 38-second loop). All the recordings new to this version are in a new, small sound pack and on the Credits page.

New: Braille display and Braille keyboard on Windows

  • As in the Windows game, through NVDA or JAWS. From the moment the sounds are loaded the game keeps the keyboard focus in a text box of its own, so the screen reader stays in focus mode and every key reaches the game; everything the game says is written into that box, so a Braille display shows it and it stays until the next message. Letters typed on a Braille keyboard, which a screen reader types as text and, in contracted Braille, as wordsigns such as have for H, are turned back into the keys they stand for. The main menu carries the Windows game's settings: Braille Keyboard (Auto, which turns itself on when text arrives the way a Braille driver sends it, On, Off), Speech (On, or Off for Braille only) and a Braille Key Test that names every key or word that arrives. Help has a General topic on it.

Fixed

  • The seabed had its own coordinates while the world above kept the world's, so sounds were misplaced there and on the ride up; a regeneration mid-ride left the next world's levels following the ride; the compass could be turned on between worlds and leave the new one muffled; help opened with the compass on played its demos muffled; a pause in a world's last two seconds let the fade run on; the water loop ran on, silent, after a ride up; and a fresh sound engine after an interruption could wire old voices into new echoes.
  • Regeneration under load. A crowd roar, a door or any other sound of the old world used to fade out with it and back in with the new one; now every sound of a world stops with that world, and anything due to play in it is dropped. The old world's echoes are switched off at once instead of running beside the new world's, and the sound engine keeps a ceiling on how many sounds play at once (the oldest short sound gives way), so a battle in full cry at the moment of a regeneration no longer piles up enough sound to overwhelm a phone.
  • From the main menu's Help, the demos of the compass beep, the low-ammo tones, the bus chime and the water slide were silent, because the game only made those tones when a run began. They are made when the game starts now.
  • Enemies no longer reach you on the bus or the water slide: chasers used to keep striking through the ride, and throwers kept throwing at the bus.

2.0.0 (8 November 2026), web only

(The description below is of the mode as 2.0.0 left it; what 3.0.0 changed is listed above.) A new mode, made for the web alone: Ultimate Mode, the hardest mode in the game, in binaural 3D sound. This is a web-only update: the Windows game is not changed, and it will not get this mode. Windows computers can now play it on the website.

New: Ultimate Mode

  • One huge, mixed world. Every run builds a world at random from five to ten areas of five kinds, each about thirty strides across, side by side: walled labyrinths, islands with a shore, sand underfoot and an elevator down to the seabed, tower areas with throwers racing you for items, battle areas with chasers, and safe zones, which sound like the places of the Windows game's events and have no enemies. Borders are open or doorways; a labyrinth always joins its neighbours through doorways, and the next area's sounds leak through each gap, heard once you are near it, so the way out is found by ear. You are dropped in silently, and nothing ever tells you where you are.
  • Survival, not speed. There is no clock. You start with 100 health and the run ends when it reaches zero; no hit ever takes more than 25, Tower's cap. Potions appear once your health drops below fifty. The first two minutes are easy, one chaser to an area; after that chaser numbers rise first, never above six in an area, enemies get slowly faster, and damage creeps up last, toward the cap. A tower area has one rival, as a tower floor does.
  • Regeneration. Every world lasts two minutes. The voice warns at fifteen seconds, Battle's countdown plays over the last five, the world fades out, and a new world is there at once, with five safe seconds. Ammo and health carry over; tower items do not.
  • Points and a survival multiplier. Kills, pickups and credits worth 40, 50 and 60 earn points, times a multiplier that rises by a half with every world survived. Nothing earns points inside a safe zone, and a credit taken there comes back out in a dangerous area.
  • Ammo without limits. You start with none. Cartridges are scattered through the world and come back after they are taken; each safe zone holds one at a time, back every fifteen seconds. From five shots left, every shot plays a low-ammo tone that drops in pitch down to one. Shots fire anywhere but in a tower area, where only thrown items count, as on a tower floor: with none in hand the attack is the empty click, never the laser, so get more items or leave the area.
  • The compass. Hold a finger still for about a second (C on a keyboard) and the world goes muffled while a centred beep points along the walkable route to the nearest safe zone: swipe to turn the compass through ahead, right, behind and left, each spoken, and the beep is highest toward the way to go, where it also says how many steps that leg runs before the route turns. Nothing moves while it is on. In a safe zone it said Compass not available. Safe zone detected (3.0.0 changes this).
  • Binaural 3D sound. Every sound in this mode is placed by the browser's HRTF panner, in front, behind, to the side or, in the elevator, below you, with the dull copy for things behind you kept on top. Each area has its own echo, sized to the area, and crossing a border blends from one echo to the next; every background rings lightly in its own room, and a safe zone's room is sized to its background, from a cathedral down to a kitchen. On the seabed under an island you hear your own breathing through the regulator, a new recording credited on the Credits page. The rest of the game is unchanged and stays in stereo.
  • Its own help section, the most detailed in the game, with demos; its own high score board and Game Stats line; Gesture Practice names the held finger.

Changed: Windows computers

  • A Windows computer may now open the web game, for Ultimate Mode only, with the keyboard. Its main menu offers that mode, High Scores, the speech rate and Leave Game (and, from 3.0.0, the Braille settings); the other modes and the events remain the Windows game's, and the play page links to its download. The start screen says how to quiet NVDA's or JAWS's speech. The home page, About, Help, the Accessibility pages and the Terms now say so.

Changed: the site

1.4.0 (6 November 2026)

A difficulty overhaul, tuned by ear, matching Windows version 2.0.0, and the backward walk as it was always meant to be.

Changed: the modes

  • Battle: the emergency cartridge, the one that appears when you run dry by firing mid-wave, now holds enough shots to finish the wave, and never fewer than three; it used to hold only enough for the enemies in the room at that moment, often one or two. Wave breaks start at 45 seconds and get a second shorter every wave, down to 30 from wave sixteen on, instead of 24 down to 10. The 60-second clock and the credit values are unchanged.
  • Tower: each tower is one and a half times as hard as the one below it, instead of twice. On tower one, floors one, two and three need one, two and three hits. A thrown item never takes more than 25 health, in tower mode and on chaos tower floors alike; it used to reach 40 by tower three and 55 deep in a climb.
  • Standard and chaos labyrinths: levels past 10 grow gently. Every labyrinth has always had a way out, but by level 20 the room was so large and the walls so dense (the shortest route averaged 36 steps) that it could not be found by ear in the time. Levels 1 to 10 are unchanged; after that the room grows half as fast and the walls thicken half as fast, never past a quarter of the cells, so level 20 averages 22 steps and level 40 about 33.
  • Events, follow the guide: in The Music Box and Whale Song the guide walks a little faster as the run goes on, so a run with no clock ends in the end: the longer you keep up, the higher your score. Published events get this without any change to their files.
  • Island, driving and the rest of chaos are unchanged.

Changed: gestures

  • Walking backwards is now a slide down and hold, the mirror of running: slide a finger down and hold it there, and you step back at once, then keep stepping at 200 steps a minute until you lift. A finger held still on the screen does nothing any more, so you can rest a finger while you play. The Walk Backwards by Holding setting and the backward-walk tick are gone. A quick swipe down is still one step back.

Changed: the site

  • A Windows update alert on the home page: for thirty days after a new Windows version is published, the home page opens with an alert saying so, with a link to the download.
