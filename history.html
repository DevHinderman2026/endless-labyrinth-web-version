Update History

What each version of the web version of Endless Labyrinth brought.

This page lists the web version. For the Windows version, see its changelog on GitHub.

The game updates by itself: the next time you open it, you have the latest version. Only a sound pack that has changed is downloaded again, and your high scores are kept. Events are not listed here: they appear in the Events menu as they open.

2.1.0 (8 November 2026), web only

Ultimate Mode after its first play tests, with water parks, buses, an arena crowd, ten new recordings, and Braille support on Windows. Web only again: the Windows game is unchanged, apart from its credits, which list the recordings the web version now carries.

Changed: Ultimate Mode

  • Renamed. The mode released in 2.0.0 as Ultimate Test Mode is now called Ultimate Mode, in the menu, in Help and across this site; this page uses the new name for 2.0.0 too. Its high scores are kept.
  • The echo, everywhere. Every area's echo was silent in 2.0.0 (a mistake in how a sound found its area's echo), and even once it rang only placed sounds reached it. Now footsteps, the countdown, the ammo chime, the laser and every other sound ring in the room you stand in, each background rings lightly in its own room, and a safe zone's room is sized to its background: a cathedral, a cave or an ice cave ring long, a kitchen, a library or a tavern are small rooms, the open air has a short light tail. Safe-zone walls ring softer. Islands and the seabed stay open air.
  • The compass speaks its leg. Turned to the way to go, it says how many steps that leg runs before the route turns ("Right, 6 steps, then turn"), or that it reaches the safe zone.
  • Bigger, quieter areas. Areas are one and a half times larger, about thirty strides across, a neighbour's background and pickups are heard only within a few strides of its border or doorway, and its enemies only once you are inside. A neighbour's air never stops and restarts as you cross borders. Inside a safe zone the world beyond its borders sounds further off, quieter and dulled as if through walls, and comes back as you step out.
  • Five minutes a world (two in 2.0.0), so a run hardens with time, and no silence between worlds: the next one is there the moment the count reaches zero. A gentler start: one chaser to an area in the first two minutes, slower chasers, two seconds between strikes, and one rival to a tower area.
  • The arena crowd. The stadium crowd has moved from the safe zones to the battle areas, where it roars under the drone and drums, and when a chaser goes down there you hear the bang and then the crowd erupts all round you (a new recording, LIVE Football Match – Stadium Crowd Cheering by arunangshubanerjee on Pixabay, two roars cut from it). When a chaser strikes you there, you hear the hit and the crowd groans or gasps (Crowd Disappointment Reaction and Crowd Shocked Reaction by Universfield on Pixabay).
  • Tower areas. Only thrown items count there: with no item in hand the attack is the empty click, never the laser, so get more items or leave the area. Items appear between four and ten strides from you, as on a tower floor, instead of anywhere in the area, and the rival's difficulty rises more slowly, its hits never above one more for every two minutes played.
  • Pickups within a stride. A cartridge, a credit, a potion or a tower item is taken from its own square or the one beside it, as Battle, Tower and Island take theirs within three units; the exact square is no longer needed, and the chime comes in the very swipe that lands on it. Cartridges are spread one to an area before any area gets two, and a beacon's echo in a long room is held to the plain room's, so one cartridge never sounds like several.
  • Water parks. A safe zone whose background is water (the ocean, a river, a waterfall, rain, a marsh, the underwater, dripping water) has a water slide, found by Tower Mode's staircase beacon. Step up to it and the ride takes over: you climb the stairs, hear the rush of the water as you slide down (a sound the game makes itself), and splash into the pool, for fifteen points times your multiplier, the one thing a safe zone pays for; the water carries you off to another part of the park, so you find the slide again by ear.
  • Buses. When a safe zone is at least forty-five steps' walk away, a bus may pull up near you (not in every world, never in a world's last minute): you hear it pull in, its air brakes hiss, a two-note chime says it has come, its engine idles where it stands, and the compass points to it while it waits. Step up to it and the doors open, you climb in and sit, the doors close, and it carries you to the nearest safe zone, a quarter of a minute from nearby and up to half a minute from the far end of the world; the doors open, you climb down, they close, and it drives off. Getting on and getting off take about three seconds each. No enemy can reach you on the bus, and the world outside is quiet and dulled through its windows. Six recordings from Pixabay, credited: Bus at bus stop (derrickmckinnon), Bus Engine Idling and INSIDE BUS (freesound_community), Bus doors open automatically and Bus doors close automatically (spinopel), Bus Leaving From a Bus Stop (freesound_community).
  • The elevator and the seabed, as in Island Mode. Swipes, the shot and the throw are silent in the car; the seabed's edge is the boundary buzz; the way up hums where it stands and your own bubbles come now and then; the ride recording is eased out by arrival and Island's three-second grace holds when you step out. New on the seabed: your own breathing through the regulator, in and out with the bubbles, for as long as you are under (Scuba diving regulator underwater breathing sound effect by ddaedalus, via freesound_community on Pixabay, a 38-second loop). All the recordings new to this version are in a new, small sound pack and on the Credits page.

New: Braille display and Braille keyboard on Windows

  • As in the Windows game, through NVDA or JAWS. From the moment the sounds are loaded the game keeps the keyboard focus in a text box of its own, so the screen reader stays in focus mode and every key reaches the game; everything the game says is written into that box, so a Braille display shows it and it stays until the next message. Letters typed on a Braille keyboard, which a screen reader types as text and, in contracted Braille, as wordsigns such as have for H, are turned back into the keys they stand for. The main menu carries the Windows game's settings: Braille Keyboard (Auto, which turns itself on when text arrives the way a Braille driver sends it, On, Off), Speech (On, or Off for Braille only) and a Braille Key Test that names every key or word that arrives. Help has a General topic on it.

Fixed

  • The seabed had its own coordinates while the world above kept the world's, so sounds were misplaced there and on the ride up; a regeneration mid-ride left the next world's levels following the ride; the compass could be turned on between worlds and leave the new one muffled; help opened with the compass on played its demos muffled; a pause in a world's last two seconds let the fade run on; the water loop ran on, silent, after a ride up; and a fresh sound engine after an interruption could wire old voices into new echoes.

2.0.0 (8 November 2026), web only

(The description below is of the mode as 2.1.0 left it; what 2.1.0 changed is listed above.) A new mode, made for the web alone: Ultimate Mode, the hardest mode in the game, in binaural 3D sound. This is a web-only update: the Windows game is not changed, and it will not get this mode. Windows computers can now play it on the website.

New: Ultimate Mode

  • One huge, mixed world. Every run builds a world at random from five to ten areas of five kinds, each about thirty strides across, side by side: walled labyrinths, islands with a shore, sand underfoot and an elevator down to the seabed, tower areas with throwers racing you for items, battle areas with chasers, and event areas with the event backgrounds, which have no enemies and are the safe zones. Borders are open or doorways; a labyrinth always joins its neighbours through doorways, and the next area's sounds leak through each gap, heard once you are near it, so the way out is found by ear. You are dropped in silently, and nothing ever tells you where you are.
  • Survival, not speed. There is no clock. You start with 100 health and the run ends when it reaches zero; no hit ever takes more than 25, Tower's cap. Potions appear once your health drops below fifty. The first two minutes are easy, one chaser to an area; after that chaser numbers rise first, never above six in an area, enemies get slowly faster, and damage creeps up last, toward the cap. A tower area has one rival, as a tower floor does.
  • Regeneration. Every world lasts five minutes. The voice warns at fifteen seconds, Battle's countdown plays over the last five, the world fades out, and a new world is there at once, with five safe seconds. Ammo and health carry over; tower items do not.
  • Points and a survival multiplier. Kills, pickups and credits worth 40, 50 and 60 earn points, times a multiplier that rises by a half with every world survived. Nothing earns points inside a safe zone, and a credit taken there comes back out in a dangerous area.
  • Ammo without limits. You start with none. Cartridges are scattered through the world and come back after they are taken; each safe zone holds one at a time, back every fifteen seconds. From five shots left, every shot plays a low-ammo tone that drops in pitch down to one. Shots fire anywhere but in a tower area, where only thrown items count, as on a tower floor: with none in hand the attack is the empty click, never the laser, so get more items or leave the area.
  • The compass. Hold a finger still for about a second (C on a keyboard) and the world goes muffled while a centred beep points along the walkable route to the nearest safe zone: swipe to turn the compass through ahead, right, behind and left, each spoken, and the beep is highest toward the way to go, where it also says how many steps that leg runs before the route turns. Nothing moves while it is on. In a safe zone it says Compass not available. Safe zone detected.
  • Binaural 3D sound. Every sound in this mode is placed by the browser's HRTF panner, in front, behind, to the side or, in the elevator, below you, with the dull copy for things behind you kept on top. Each area has its own echo, sized to the area, and crossing a border blends from one echo to the next; every background rings lightly in its own room, and a safe zone's room is sized to its background, from a cathedral down to a kitchen. On the seabed under an island you hear your own breathing through the regulator, a new recording credited on the Credits page. The rest of the game is unchanged and stays in stereo.
  • Its own help section, the most detailed in the game, with demos; its own high score board and Game Stats line; Gesture Practice names the held finger.

Changed: Windows computers

  • A Windows computer may now open the web game, for Ultimate Mode only, with the keyboard. Its main menu offers that mode, High Scores, the speech rate and Leave Game (and, from 2.1.0, the Braille settings); the other modes and the events remain the Windows game's, and the play page links to its download. The start screen says how to quiet NVDA's or JAWS's speech. The home page, About, Help, the Accessibility pages and the Terms now say so.

Changed: the site

  • The Story of the Game has a section on Ultimate Mode, a line saying the page was written with Claude's help from Russell's own words and decisions, and a correction: the very first version of Endless Labyrinth was made in Google Gemini, before the work carried on in Claude.

1.4.0 (6 November 2026)

A difficulty overhaul, tuned by ear, matching Windows version 2.0.0, and the backward walk as it was always meant to be.

Changed: the modes

  • Battle: the emergency cartridge, the one that appears when you run dry by firing mid-wave, now holds enough shots to finish the wave, and never fewer than three; it used to hold only enough for the enemies in the room at that moment, often one or two. Wave breaks start at 45 seconds and get a second shorter every wave, down to 30 from wave sixteen on, instead of 24 down to 10. The 60-second clock and the credit values are unchanged.
  • Tower: each tower is one and a half times as hard as the one below it, instead of twice. On tower one, floors one, two and three need one, two and three hits. A thrown item never takes more than 25 health, in tower mode and on chaos tower floors alike; it used to reach 40 by tower three and 55 deep in a climb.
  • Standard and chaos labyrinths: levels past 10 grow gently. Every labyrinth has always had a way out, but by level 20 the room was so large and the walls so dense (the shortest route averaged 36 steps) that it could not be found by ear in the time. Levels 1 to 10 are unchanged; after that the room grows half as fast and the walls thicken half as fast, never past a quarter of the cells, so level 20 averages 22 steps and level 40 about 33.
  • Events, follow the guide: in The Music Box and Whale Song the guide walks a little faster as the run goes on, so a run with no clock ends in the end: the longer you keep up, the higher your score. Published events get this without any change to their files.
  • Island, driving and the rest of chaos are unchanged.

Changed: gestures

  • Walking backwards is now a slide down and hold, the mirror of running: slide a finger down and hold it there, and you step back at once, then keep stepping at 200 steps a minute until you lift. A finger held still on the screen does nothing any more, so you can rest a finger while you play. The Walk Backwards by Holding setting and the backward-walk tick are gone. A quick swipe down is still one step back.

Changed: the site

  • A Windows update alert on the home page: for thirty days after a new Windows version is published, the home page opens with an alert saying so, with a link to the download. It sits in a region named Alert at the very top of the page: a screen reader says "Alert, region" when it reaches it and lists it among the landmarks, and it never interrupts what you are reading.
  • The Terms of Use, Privacy Policy and Accessibility Statement are rewritten in a formal register, with defined terms, and now describe how the Windows version updates itself at the player's request.
  • The Help page's questions explain how each version updates.

1.3.0 (5 November 2026)

New

  • A Help page, in the site menu: the game's whole help, every topic in the game's own order and word for word, so you can read it all without starting the game. Tap a topic to open it, and its subtopics follow, each under its own heading. Every button has a screen reader hint that says what it will do before you tap it.
  • Every sound demo plays on the page: each one has a Play button, and plays in the same room as in the game's help. Only that one sound is downloaded, the first time; if you have played the game on this device, its sounds are already there.
  • The help uses gestures on a phone or tablet and keys on a computer, as the game does.

Changed

  • The frequently asked questions are now the first topic on the Help page. The old questions page sends you there, so old links still work.
  • In the game's help, the event item sounds have their own section, Demos, Events, after the other demos, as on the Help page. Event topics that explain a sound, such as the bell and the mission sounds, stay in Events.

1.2.0 (5 November 2026)

New

  • Running: on a phone or tablet, slide a finger up and hold it there to run. You step forward at once, then keep stepping, fast, at 280 steps a minute, for as long as your finger stays on the screen. No more swiping up over and over.
  • Walking backwards: hold a finger still on the screen for half a second, and you step back, then keep stepping, more slowly, at 200 steps a minute, until you lift your finger.
  • Only lifting your finger stops you, and you also stop by yourself at a wall, at the edge of an arena, and whenever a new level begins, so you never run blindly into a new room. Turning is unchanged: lift your finger, then swipe left or right. Lift it, too, before you shoot or tap.
  • Both work in every mode and in events. Driving keeps its own accelerator, and in an event where up and down are answers rather than steps, holding does not repeat them. Computers keep the keyboard as before.
  • A soft tick tells you when a still finger has started walking you backwards.
  • A new main menu setting, Walk Backwards by Holding, turns the backward walk off for anyone who rests a finger on the screen while playing; a still finger then does nothing, and running and swipes work as before. Backups carry the setting.
  • Gesture Practice names both, and help and About and how to play explain them.

Changed

  • During a run, a finger resting still on the screen now walks you backwards, so it is no longer a place to rest a finger, unless you turn Walk Backwards by Holding off.

1.1.2 (5 November 2026)

Changed

  • The Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy now cover the Windows version too, so one set of terms applies to both versions. The Terms have a new section 23 for the Windows version alone: download it only from the official Releases page, do not change the program, its save files are sealed, and what a Windows security warning means. Copies are in the Windows version's repository.
  • The Terms now say plainly that you are free to make your own game from scratch, and that changing Endless Labyrinth itself or sharing a version built from it needs the maker's written permission. Ideas and improvements are welcome.
  • The Privacy Policy lists exactly what the Windows version contacts, and why.

1.1.1 (5 November 2026)

Fixed

  • The footer showed a stray heading, More about this site, above its links. It is gone; the links sit under the contact lines.
  • On a phone, the Site links menu button was read aloud with a triangle symbol after its name. It now says only Site links menu.
  • Game Stats said that the game on an iPhone's Home Screen and the game in Safari share their scores. They do not: each keeps its own. Game Stats, Score Backups and the questions page now say so, and explain how to see the Home Screen copy's stats and move scores between the two with a backup.
  • The questions page said that every event already seen stays available without the internet. Only the built-in events do, and the page now says so.
  • Sharing a page from a link to one of its sections shared that section's address; the share buttons now share the page itself.

1.1.0 (4 November 2026)

The website grows into a home for the game: new pages for events, stats, backups and accessibility, the site's terms, and a fix for phones and iPads. The game's modes, sounds and rules are unchanged, and the Windows version is unchanged.

New

  • Events calendar: a page listing every event, on now, coming soon and past, with its dates in your own time zone, its description, and your own best run, medal and number of runs in it. It reads the same signed event files as the game and judges what is on by internet time, so it always agrees with the game.
  • Game Stats: your time played and runs per mode (counted from this version on), your best score on every board, your medals, and every event you have played. An event that is no longer published is kept, marked as no longer available, as the Windows version keeps its Event High Scores.
  • Score Backups: Back up now saves a sealed file of every score, your stats and your settings, to keep in Files, iCloud Drive or Google Drive. Restore reads it back and always asks first: combine it with what is on the device, keeping the better of everything, replace, or cancel. An edited file is refused.
  • Accessibility page: display options for low-vision players (high contrast and bigger text, on every page and in the game), the screen reader named for your device, and a setup guide for each device: iPhone and iPad, Android, Chromebook, Mac, Linux and Windows.
  • Terms of Use, Privacy Policy and Accessibility Statement, in the footer of every page, each with the date it was last updated. The Terms say plainly that recording and streaming the game is encouraged, with YouTube's dispute and appeal steps explained, and that the game is free and stays free.
  • Frequently asked questions, in the footer: the website and Windows versions compared, headphones, screen readers, scores, the internet and events.
  • Share buttons in the footer, for Facebook, X, Reddit, WhatsApp and email, plus Copy the link and your device's own share menu. Each service's page opens only when you tap its button; nothing of theirs loads with the page.
  • The site links now include Play, Events, Game Stats, Score Backups and Accessibility. On a narrow screen they fold behind a Site links menu button, which tells a screen reader whether it is expanded or collapsed; on a wide screen every link shows.

Fixed

  • An iPhone could be taken for an iPad, and an iPad for a phone, so the game named the wrong device when it asked for silent mode to be off. The game now tells them apart by the size of the screen, which no browser setting changes.

Changed

  • Saved scores move to format 2, which adds time played and runs per mode. Every score and setting from before is kept.

  • Links to the Windows version's download and its changelog, from the story and from this page.

Changed

  • The website's lists and tables read more cleanly on a phone's narrow screen.

1.0.3 (3 November 2026)

New

  • The web version now also plays on a Chromebook, a Mac or a Linux computer, with the keyboard: the same keys as the Windows version. On a computer, everything the game says names the keys instead of gestures, including help and event descriptions. Chrome, Edge or Safari is recommended. Windows computers keep the full Windows version.

1.0.2 (3 November 2026)

Fixed

  • On an iPhone, Chaos mode could lose all its sound a few levels into a run, usually in the third battle. The game now works out its room echoes in a way iPhones handle without trouble.

Changed

  • Gesture Practice now says only what each gesture felt, without the testing information it used to add.

1.0.1 (3 November 2026)

The complete game: every mode and the events, with the same rules, timings and sounds as the Windows version.

New

  • Driving mode: a three-lane road, played by holding a finger on the screen to accelerate. Two high scores: cars destroyed and distance.
  • Tower mode: climb towers floor by floor against a rival.
  • Island mode: a chain of islands, crossed by the seabed.
  • Chaos mode: every kind of level, chosen at random, against one five-minute clock.
  • Events and Event High Scores: the same limited-time challenges as the Windows version. Event descriptions name the gestures to use.
  • Help covers every mode, with demo sections for every sound.
  • Phones have no T key, so in Driving, and in Chaos's battles, roads, tower floors and islands, a double tap's report also gives the seconds left on the clock.

Fixed

  • On an iPhone, the game's sounds stayed silent while its voice worked. They now play together.
  • Footsteps were sometimes cut short, or dipped in volume, while walking.
  • On some phones the game could close in the middle of a long Chaos run. It now uses much less memory.
  • Echoes in the rooms now behave exactly as on Windows: they stop, pause and fade with the sound they belong to, and are never louder than on Windows.
  • Large Android tablets that open websites in desktop mode are now let in. Computers are still not supported.

Changed

  • The first download is now about 120 megabytes, since it holds the sounds of every mode and the events.

1.0.0 (2 November 2026)

The first release of the web version, for iPhone, iPad and Android phones and tablets.

  • Standard mode and Battle mode, converted from the Windows game with the same rules, timings and sounds.
  • Touch gestures for every action: swipes to move, a double tap to choose, a two-finger tap to attack, a three-finger tap to pause, and a Gesture Practice to learn them. A Bluetooth keyboard works too.
  • The game speaks for itself, with a Speech Rate setting.
  • Stereo sound with the Windows game's room echo and its duller sound for anything behind you.
  • High scores kept on your device, and the game can be added to your Home Screen.
  • Help with demo sections for every sound.
